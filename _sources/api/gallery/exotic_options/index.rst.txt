Examples
========

This gallery walks through every public feature of
``financialkit.exotic_options``: barrier options from Merton's image
formula to discrete monitoring, binary options, Asian options,
lookbacks and variance swaps, compound, forward-start and chooser
options, options on two assets, and American options by least-squares
Monte Carlo. Each script illustrates one dated breakthrough from the
:doc:`history page </history/exotic_options_breakthroughs>`.

Each script in this gallery is self-contained and can be run directly with
``python examples/exotic_options/<section>/<script>.py``.

Sections
--------

- **barriers** -- Merton's down-and-out call, the Reiner-Rubinstein
  formulas, Ikeda-Kunitomo double barriers and the Broadie-Glasserman-Kou
  correction for discrete monitoring.
- **binaries** -- cash-or-nothing, asset-or-nothing, gap and one-touch
  options.
- **asian** -- Kemna-Vorst geometric averages and control variates, and
  Turnbull-Wakeman moment matching.
- **path_dependent** -- floating and fixed lookbacks and variance swaps.
- **compound** -- Geske compound options, forward starts and choosers.
- **multi_asset** -- Margrabe exchange options, Stulz options on the
  maximum and minimum, and quantos.
- **american** -- Longstaff-Schwartz least-squares Monte Carlo.

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Monte Carlo simulates forward, but early exercise is decided backward: whether to exercise today depends on the value of waiting. Francis Longstaff and Eduardo Schwartz (2001) estimated that continuation value by regressing the discounted future cash flows of the in-the-money paths on a few functions of today&#x27;s price. Working back from expiry, each path is exercised where the payoff beats the fitted value. The method extends to many assets and path-dependent payoffs, where trees and PDEs give up.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="An average-rate option pays on the average price over its life, which makes it cheaper than a vanilla option and harder to manipulate at expiry. Angelien Kemna and Ton Vorst (1990) observed that a geometric average of lognormal prices is lognormal, with volatility \sigma/\sqrt3 under continuous averaging, so its option has a Black-Scholes formula. Binary options
--------------

All-or-nothing payoffs, the building blocks into which Reiner and
Rubinstein decomposed vanilla and barrier options.

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Mark Rubinstein and Eric Reiner&#x27;s &quot;Unscrambling the Binary Code&quot; (1991) priced the all-or-nothing payoffs and showed that every vanilla and barrier option is assembled from them. A cash-or-nothing call pays 1 if S_T &gt; K and is worth e^{-rT}N(d_2); an asset-or-nothing call pays the share and is worth Se^{-qT}N(d_1). A vanilla call is one asset-or-nothing call minus K cash-or-nothing calls. A gap option uses different trigger and payoff strikes and can be worth less than nothing, and a one-touch pays as soon as a barrier is hit.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="William Margrabe (1978) priced the right to exchange one asset for another, (S_1 - S_2)^+. Measured in units of the second asset, it is a call on the ratio S_1/S_2 struck at 1, so the interest rate drops out and only the volatility of the ratio, \sigma^2 = \sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2 - 2\rho\sigma_1\sigma_2, matters. Lookbacks and variance swaps
----------------------------

Payoffs on the extremes of the path, and on its realized variance:
floating and fixed-strike lookbacks, and the replication of a variance
swap by a strip of options.


.. raw:: html

  <div id='sg-tag-list' class='sphx-glr-tag-list'></div>


.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbnails">

.. thumbnail-parent-div-open

.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="&quot;Buy at the low, sell at the high&quot;: Barry Goldman, Howard Sosin and Mary Ann Gatto (1979) priced the option whose strike is the lowest price over its life, S_T - \min_t S_t (or the highest, for the put). It is never out of the money, so it costs much more than a vanilla call. The joint law of a Brownian motion and its minimum gives the closed form; Monte Carlo can match it exactly only by sampling each step&#x27;s minimum from the Brownian bridge, because checking the price at discrete dates misses the true low.">

Once the running maximum has passed the strike, the call has locked in \max - K and what remains is a call on any further rise; that is how a seasoned option is priced, with the maximum observed so far as an input.">
