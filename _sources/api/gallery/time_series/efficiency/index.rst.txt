    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Maurice Kendall (1953) set out to find the cycles in 22 British price series (wheat, cotton, share indices) at weekly intervals. He found none: the serial correlations of the price changes were indistinguishable from zero, and &quot;the series looks like a wandering one, almost as if once a week the Demon of Chance drew a random number&quot;. The levels, by contrast, are almost perfectly autocorrelated, which is what makes them look patterned to the eye.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Correlation coefficients can be dominated by a few large changes. In his 1965 thesis Eugene Fama added a distribution-free check on the 30 Dow Jones stocks: count the runs, the unbroken sequences of price changes with the same sign. Positive dependence produces long runs and too few of them; reversals produce too many. Under independence the number of runs is approximately normal with a known mean and variance, and Fama found the observed counts close to the expected ones.">
