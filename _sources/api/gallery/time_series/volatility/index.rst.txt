    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="GARCH models fit beautifully in-sample, yet regressions of squared daily returns on their variance forecasts gave R^2 of a few percent, and sceptics concluded the models were useless for forecasting. Torben Andersen and Tim Bollerslev (1998) showed that the fault lay with the yardstick. A squared return \sigma_t^2 z_t^2 is an unbiased but very noisy measure of the day&#x27;s variance, and even a perfect forecast explains only a small part of it. Summing squared five-minute returns (realized variance) measures the variance almost exactly, and against it the same forecasts explain many times more.">
