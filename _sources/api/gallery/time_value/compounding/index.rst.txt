Compounding
-----------

Interest on interest: Pacioli's rule of 72, Witt's compound-interest
tables, and Bernoulli's limit of ever more frequent compounding.

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Luca Pacioli&#x27;s Summa de arithmetica (1494) gives, without proof, a rule for how long money takes to double: divide 72 by the annual interest rate in percent. The exact doubling time with annual compounding is \ln 2 / \ln(1 + r), and since \ln(1 + r) \approx r - r^2/2 the best constant drifts upward with the rate; 72 is close to right near 8% and has many convenient divisors.">

For a given rate it listed, year by year, the amount of 1, the present value of 1, and the value of an annuity of 1, so that a clerk could price a loan or a lease with a look-up and a multiplication. This script prints such a table at 10% and uses it to price an annuity.">

At 100% a year, 1 grows to (1 + 1/m)^m with m compounding periods, which increases with m but stays bounded; he showed the limit lies between 2.5 and 3. The limit is e, and continuous compounding, e^{rt}, is the convention financialkit uses by default.">
