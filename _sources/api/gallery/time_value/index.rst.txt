Examples
========

This gallery walks through every public feature of ``financialkit.time_value``:
compounding and equivalent rates, present and net present value, annuities
and perpetuities, the internal rate of return and its pitfalls, amortizing
loans and the APR, and day-count conventions.

Each script in this gallery is self-contained and can be run directly with
``python examples/time_value/<section>/<script>.py``.

Sections
--------

- **present_value** -- Fibonacci's present value, Fisher's real rate and
  Fisher's net present value.
- **compounding** -- Pacioli's rule of 72, Witt's compound-interest tables
  and Bernoulli's continuous compounding.
- **annuities** -- De Witt's life annuities, the consol perpetuity and
  Price's sinking fund.
- **irr** -- the internal rate of return, Lorie and Savage's multiple IRRs
  and Baldwin's modified IRR.
- **amortization** -- the fully amortizing mortgage and the
  Truth-in-Lending APR.
- **day_counts** -- 30/360 accrued interest and the actual/actual family.

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Luca Pacioli&#x27;s Summa de arithmetica (1494) gives, without proof, a rule for how long money takes to double: divide 72 by the annual interest rate in percent. The exact doubling time with annual compounding is \ln 2 / \ln(1 + r), and since \ln(1 + r) \approx r - r^2/2 the best constant drifts upward with the rate; 72 is close to right near 8% and has many convenient divisors.">

For a given rate it listed, year by year, the amount of 1, the present value of 1, and the value of an annuity of 1, so that a clerk could price a loan or a lease with a look-up and a multiplication. Internal rate of return
-----------------------

The rate that zeroes a project's NPV, the projects that have several,
and Baldwin's modified IRR.


.. raw:: html

  <div id='sg-tag-list' class='sphx-glr-tag-list'></div>


.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbnails">

.. thumbnail-parent-div-open

.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Irving Fisher&#x27;s The Theory of Interest (1930) defined the &quot;rate of return over cost&quot;: the discount rate at which an investment&#x27;s net present value is zero, now called the internal rate of return. For a conventional project (one outflow followed by inflows) the NPV falls steadily as the rate rises and crosses zero exactly once, so the IRR is well defined and accepting projects with IRR above the cost of capital is the same as accepting positive-NPV ones. financialkit brackets the crossing on a grid and polishes it with scipy.optimize.brentq.">

.. only:: html

  .. image:: /api/gallery/time_value/irr/images/thumb/sphx_glr_plot_01_internal_rate_of_return_thumb.png

.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="In &quot;Three Problems in Rationing Capital&quot; (Journal of Business, 1955) James Lorie and Leonard Savage pointed out that the IRR can fail. Their example: replacing an oil-well pump with a bigger one costs 1,600 now, brings in 10,000 extra next year, and loses the 10,000 the old pump would have produced the year after. The NPV is zero at both 25% and 400%, and negative outside them. By Descartes&#x27; rule of signs a stream with k sign changes can have up to k IRRs; irr_all finds every one as a root of the NPV polynomial in 1/(1 + r), and irr warns when there is more than one.">

  .. image:: /api/gallery/time_value/irr/images/thumb/sphx_glr_plot_02_lorie_savage_multiple_irr_thumb.png

.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Robert Baldwin&#x27;s &quot;How to Assess Investment Proposals&quot; (Harvard Business Review, 1959) proposed a fix for the IRR&#x27;s two defects -- multiple roots and the implicit assumption that interim cash flows are reinvested at the IRR itself. Compound the inflows to the horizon at a realistic reinvestment rate, discount the outflows at the finance rate, and report the one rate that links them: \mathrm{MIRR} = (FV^+ / |PV^-|)^{1/n} - 1. It always exists and is unique.">

Present value
-------------

Discounting payments to one comparable number: Fibonacci's present
value, Fisher's real and nominal rates, and Fisher's net present value.

.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Chapter 12 of Fibonacci&#x27;s Liber Abaci (1202) contains problems in which a sum is repaid in instalments at a fixed rate per period, and Fibonacci values each schedule by discounting every instalment back to the start (Goetzmann, 2005). The same idea in modern form: a lender is offered three ways to be repaid a loan of 100 bezants, and the one worth most today depends on the rate. Present value turns each schedule into a single number that can be compared.">
