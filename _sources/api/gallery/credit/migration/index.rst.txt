    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="J. P. Morgan&#x27;s CreditMetrics (1997) revalues a bond in every rating it could hold a year from now, weights the values with a rating transition matrix, and reads the credit VaR off the resulting distribution. For a portfolio, obligors migrate jointly: each one&#x27;s asset return is mapped to a rating through normal thresholds, and the asset returns are correlated. The Technical Document&#x27;s five-year BBB bond has mean value 107.09 and standard deviation 2.99.">

A generator \Lambda, with non-negative off-diagonal intensities and rows summing to zero, gives the transition matrix for every horizon, P(t) = e^{\Lambda t}. Default probabilities by rating, and risky bond prices with recovery of treasury, follow for any maturity, including fractions of a year that an annual matrix cannot provide.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Going from an annual transition matrix P to a generator is not as simple as \log P. Israel, Rosenthal and Wei (2001) showed that for empirical rating matrices the principal logarithm usually has negative off-diagonal entries, which would be negative migration intensities. Their fix: set those entries to zero and absorb the change in the diagonal. A weighted variant spreads it over the row instead. Both leave e^{G} within a few basis points of P.">
