Examples
========

This gallery walks through every public feature of ``financialkit.credit``:
Altman's Z-score; Merton's structural model, Black-Cox first passage,
Leland's endogenous default and the KMV distance to default; hazard
rates and recovery conventions; CDS pricing and hazard bootstrapping;
rating migration with CreditMetrics and generator matrices; and
portfolio credit risk with Vasicek's large pool, CreditRisk+, Li's
copula, the Basel IRB formula and CDO tranches.

See also the narrative tutorial:

- :doc:`/tutorials/from_one_firm_to_a_portfolio`

Each script in this gallery is self-contained and can be run directly with
``python examples/credit/<section>/<script>.py``.

Sections
--------

- **scoring** -- Altman's Z-score.
- **structural** -- Merton, Black-Cox, Leland and the distance to default.
- **reduced_form** -- Jarrow-Turnbull hazard rates and Duffie-Singleton
  recovery of market value.
- **cds** -- the CDS par spread and the ISDA hazard bootstrap.
- **migration** -- CreditMetrics, generator matrices and the
  Israel-Rosenthal-Wei regularization.
- **portfolio_loss** -- Vasicek's large pool, CreditRisk+, Li's Gaussian
  copula, the Basel IRB formula and tranche expected losses.

.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="J. P. Morgan&#x27;s CreditMetrics (1997) revalues a bond in every rating it could hold a year from now, weights the values with a rating transition matrix, and reads the credit VaR off the resulting distribution. For a portfolio, obligors migrate jointly: each one&#x27;s asset return is mapped to a rating through normal thresholds, and the asset returns are correlated. The Technical Document&#x27;s five-year BBB bond has mean value 107.09 and standard deviation 2.99.">

.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Vasicek (1987) let every loan default when its asset return \sqrt\rho M + \sqrt{1-\rho}\,\varepsilon_i falls below N^{-1}(p). Given the common factor M, defaults are independent, so in a large pool the default rate is the deterministic p(M), and its distribution is known in closed form. Correlation does not change the expected loss, only how it is spread out: at high correlation most years are quiet and a few are disasters.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Jarrow and Turnbull (1995) modelled the timing of default rather than its cause: default is the first jump of a Poisson process with intensity \lambda(t), so the survival probability is Q(t) = e^{-\int_0^t \lambda}. A risky zero-coupon bond that recovers a fraction \delta of a riskless one is worth P(0,T)[\delta + (1-\delta)Q(T)], and its spread is close to \lambda(1 - \delta): the credit triangle.">

Structural models

Default as an option on the firm's assets: at maturity, at a barrier,
or when shareholders choose.

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Merton (1974) treated a firm&#x27;s equity as a European call on its assets V, struck at the face value D of its zero-coupon debt. Debt is then a riskless bond minus a put, the risk-neutral default probability is N(-d_2), and the credit spread follows from leverage and asset volatility alone. Hull&#x27;s Example 24.3 backs out the unobserved asset value and volatility from equity (3) and its volatility (80%).">
