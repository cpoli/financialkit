    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Jarrow and Turnbull (1995) modelled the timing of default rather than its cause: default is the first jump of a Poisson process with intensity \lambda(t), so the survival probability is Q(t) = e^{-\int_0^t \lambda}. A risky zero-coupon bond that recovers a fraction \delta of a riskless one is worth P(0,T)[\delta + (1-\delta)Q(T)], and its spread is close to \lambda(1 - \delta): the credit triangle.">
