His hierarchical risk parity never inverts it: it clusters assets by the correlation distance \sqrt{(1-\rho_{ij})/2}, reorders them along the dendrogram so similar assets sit together, then splits capital top down between halves in inverse proportion to their variance. Its risk varies less from one estimation sample to the next than the minimum-variance portfolio&#x27;s, though with few observations per asset minimum variance can still be less risky on average.">
