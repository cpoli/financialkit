Examples
========

This gallery walks through every public feature of
``financialkit.monte_carlo``: crude Monte Carlo with error bars, variance
reduction, quasi-Monte Carlo, Monte Carlo Greeks and multilevel Monte
Carlo. Each script illustrates one dated breakthrough from the
:doc:`history page </history/monte_carlo_breakthroughs>`.

See also the narrative tutorial:

- :doc:`/tutorials/one_option_five_pricers`

Each script in this gallery is self-contained and can be run directly with
``python examples/monte_carlo/<section>/<script>.py``.

Sections
--------

- **basics** -- the Monte Carlo method of Metropolis and Ulam, Box-Muller
  normal variates and Boyle's option prices.
- **variance_reduction** -- Neyman stratification, importance sampling,
  antithetic variates, Latin hypercube sampling, control variates and
  moment matching.
- **quasi_monte_carlo** -- Halton and Sobol sequences, the Koksma-Hlawka
  inequality, Owen scrambling, Paskov-Traub's high-dimensional
  integrals and the Brownian bridge.
- **greeks** -- likelihood-ratio and pathwise Greeks.
- **multilevel** -- Giles's multilevel Monte Carlo.

Quasi-Monte Carlo
-----------------

Low-discrepancy points instead of random ones: Halton and Sobol
sequences, the Koksma-Hlawka error bound, randomized QMC with error bars,
and high-dimensional path integrals with and without the Brownian bridge.

John Halton (1960) took one such radical-inverse sequence per coordinate, in distinct prime bases, to fill the d-dimensional cube. The points are deterministic, yet integrate far better than random ones in low dimension, with an error close to (\log n)^d / n.">
