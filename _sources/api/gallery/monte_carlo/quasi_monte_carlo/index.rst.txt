Quasi-Monte Carlo
-----------------

Low-discrepancy points instead of random ones: Halton and Sobol
sequences, the Koksma-Hlawka error bound, randomized QMC with error bars,
and high-dimensional path integrals with and without the Brownian bridge.

John Halton (1960) took one such radical-inverse sequence per coordinate, in distinct prime bases, to fill the d-dimensional cube. The points are deterministic, yet integrate far better than random ones in low dimension, with an error close to (\log n)^d / n.">

Art Owen (1995) randomly permuted the digits of a net, nested so that the result is still a net: every scrambled point is uniform on the cube, so the estimate is unbiased, independent scramblings give an honest standard error, and for smooth integrands the root-mean-square error falls like N^{-3/2}, faster than the unscrambled sequence&#x27;s N^{-1}.">

Spassimir Paskov and Joseph Traub (1995) priced a Goldman Sachs collateralized mortgage obligation, a 360-dimensional integral, and found Sobol points beat Monte Carlo anyway.
