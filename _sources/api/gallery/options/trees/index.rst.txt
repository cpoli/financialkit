Lattices and American options
-----------------------------

Binomial and trinomial trees, their convergence to Black-Scholes, early
exercise of American options, and an analytic approximation to it.

.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="John Cox, Stephen Ross and Mark Rubinstein (1979) showed that the Black-Scholes price is the limit of a much simpler model: in each short period the stock moves up by u = e^{\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}} or down by d = 1/u, and the option is replicated one step at a time. The risk-neutral probability p = (e^{r\Delta t} - d)/(u - d) falls out of the replication, and backward induction prices any payoff. The error shrinks like 1/n, oscillating as the strike moves between nodes.">

At each node an American option is worth the larger of its continuation value and its immediate exercise value, so backward induction prices it as easily as a European option. For a put, early exercise is optimal once the stock falls below a critical price S^*(t) that rises towards the strike as expiry approaches.">

With u = e^{\lambda\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}}, d = 1/u and a stretch \lambda &gt; 1, the three probabilities are chosen to match the risk-neutral mean and variance of the next price exactly. The extra branch makes the lattice smoother: for the same number of steps the trinomial tree is usually more accurate than the binomial one.">

The early-exercise premium solves the Black-Scholes PDE; dropping one small time-derivative term leaves an ordinary differential equation with power-law solutions, and smooth pasting at a critical price S^* pins down the coefficient. One scalar root replaces a whole lattice.">

Instead of fixing u and d from the volatility, they choose the probabilities so that the tree&#x27;s cumulative binomial distribution reproduces Black-Scholes&#x27;s N(d_2) and N(d_1), using the Peizer-Pratt inversion of the normal onto the binomial. The tree is centred on the strike, and European prices converge smoothly at order 1/n^2.">
