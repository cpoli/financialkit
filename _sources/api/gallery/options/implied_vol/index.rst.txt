Implied volatility
------------------

Reading volatilities and distributions back out of option prices: the
implied volatility, its at-the-money shortcut, the smile, and the
risk-neutral density.

Henry Latané and Richard Rendleman (1976) turned the formula around: given a market price, solve for the volatility that reproduces it. The price is strictly increasing in \sigma between Merton&#x27;s bounds, so the root is unique; it exists only for prices strictly inside them.">

Before October 1987 index options roughly agreed; after the crash, low-strike puts became permanently more expensive, and implied volatility fell steeply with the strike (Rubinstein 1994). Inverting a strip of option prices strike by strike shows the market&#x27;s departure from the lognormal. Here the prices come from two simple arbitrage-free models: a mixture of volatilities (a symmetric smile) and a mixture of a normal regime with a crash regime (a skew).">
