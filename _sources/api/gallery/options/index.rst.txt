Examples
========

This gallery walks through every public feature of ``financialkit.options``:
the closed-form pricers from Bachelier to Garman-Kohlhagen, put-call
parity and the no-arbitrage bounds, the Greeks and delta hedging,
implied volatility, the smile and the risk-neutral density, and the
binomial and trinomial trees with American early exercise.

See also the narrative tutorials:

- :doc:`/tutorials/one_option_five_pricers`
- :doc:`/tutorials/where_the_smile_comes_from`

Each script in this gallery is self-contained and can be run directly with
``python examples/options/<section>/<script>.py``.

Sections
--------

- **closed_form** -- Bachelier, Stoll's put-call parity, Black-Scholes,
  Merton's dividends and bounds, Black-76 and Garman-Kohlhagen.
- **greeks** -- the Black-Scholes Greeks and discrete delta hedging.
- **implied_vol** -- implied volatility, the Breeden-Litzenberger density,
  the volatility smile and the Brenner-Subrahmanyam approximation.
- **trees** -- the Cox-Ross-Rubinstein, Rendleman-Bartter, Boyle
  trinomial and Leisen-Reimer lattices, and the Barone-Adesi-Whaley
  American approximation.

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Every input to Black-Scholes is observable except the volatility. Henry Latané and Richard Rendleman (1976) turned the formula around: given a market price, solve for the volatility that reproduces it. The price is strictly increasing in \sigma between Merton&#x27;s bounds, so the root is unique; it exists only for prices strictly inside them.">

Lattices and American options
-----------------------------

Binomial and trinomial trees, their convergence to Black-Scholes, early
exercise of American options, and an analytic approximation to it.


.. raw:: html

  <div id='sg-tag-list' class='sphx-glr-tag-list'></div>


.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbnails">

.. thumbnail-parent-div-open

.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="John Cox, Stephen Ross and Mark Rubinstein (1979) showed that the Black-Scholes price is the limit of a much simpler model: in each short period the stock moves up by u = e^{\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}} or down by d = 1/u, and the option is replicated one step at a time. The risk-neutral probability p = (e^{r\Delta t} - d)/(u - d) falls out of the replication, and backward induction prices any payoff. The error shrinks like 1/n, oscillating as the strike moves between nodes.">

.. only:: html

  .. image:: /api/gallery/options/trees/images/thumb/sphx_glr_plot_01_crr_convergence_thumb.png
    :alt:

  :doc:`/api/gallery/options/trees/plot_01_crr_convergence`

.. raw:: html

      <div class="sphx-glr-thumbnail-title">Cox-Ross-Rubinstein: the binomial tree converges to Black-Scholes</div>
    </div>


.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="The binomial tree&#x27;s real advantage over a closed form is early exercise. At each node an American option is worth the larger of its continuation value and its immediate exercise value, so backward induction prices it as easily as a European option. For a put, early exercise is optimal once the stock falls below a critical price S^*(t) that rises towards the strike as expiry approaches.">

.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Phelim Boyle (1986) added a third, flat branch to the binomial step. With u = e^{\lambda\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}}, d = 1/u and a stretch \lambda &gt; 1, the three probabilities are chosen to match the risk-neutral mean and variance of the next price exactly. The extra branch makes the lattice smoother: for the same number of steps the trinomial tree is usually more accurate than the binomial one.">

The early-exercise premium solves the Black-Scholes PDE; dropping one small time-derivative term leaves an ordinary differential equation with power-law solutions, and smooth pasting at a critical price S^* pins down the coefficient. One scalar root replaces a whole lattice.">

Instead of fixing u and d from the volatility, they choose the probabilities so that the tree&#x27;s cumulative binomial distribution reproduces Black-Scholes&#x27;s N(d_2) and N(d_1), using the Peizer-Pratt inversion of the normal onto the binomial. The tree is centred on the strike, and European prices converge smoothly at order 1/n^2.">
