Schemes
-------

The Black-Scholes equation on a grid: explicit, implicit and
Crank-Nicolson time stepping, the tridiagonal solves they need, the
transformation to the heat equation, the link to trinomial trees, and
knock-out barriers.

Every step is then a linear solve, but no step is too large: the scheme is stable and monotone for any \Delta\tau, at the price of first-order accuracy in time. ImplicitScheme shows both sides: a large step that would wreck the explicit scheme gives a sensible answer, and halving the step halves the error.">

.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Every implicit step in one space dimension is a tridiagonal system. Llewellyn Thomas (1949) wrote down the specialised Gaussian elimination that solves it in O(m) operations: one sweep removes the sub-diagonal, a second substitutes back. No pivoting is needed because the implicit Black-Scholes matrix is diagonally dominant. thomas_solve is that algorithm by hand; it agrees with scipy.linalg.solve_banded to rounding, and its cost grows linearly while a dense solve grows cubically.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Black and Scholes (1973) found their formula by changing variables until the pricing equation became the heat equation. With x = \ln(S/K) and \tau = \tfrac12\sigma^2(T - t), V = K e^{\alpha x + \beta\tau} u turns Black-Scholes into u_\tau = u_{xx}, whose initial condition is the transformed payoff. heat_equation_price solves the heat equation by Crank-Nicolson on a uniform grid, watches the payoff diffuse, and transforms back.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Brennan and Schwartz (1978) noticed that in x = \ln S the explicit scheme&#x27;s weights on the three neighbouring nodes sum to one and match the mean and variance of the log-price increment: the explicit scheme is a trinomial tree, and its stability condition is just the requirement that the weights be probabilities. With \Delta x = \sigma\sqrt{3\Delta t} the middle weight is 2/3, Hull&#x27;s standard tree. log_explicit_price runs the scheme, and explicit_trinomial_probabilities shows the weights turn negative exactly where the scheme becomes unstable.">

.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="A knock-out option is a boundary condition: the value is zero on the barrier. Zvan, Forsyth and Vetzal (2000) showed that finite differences price barriers accurately when a grid node sits exactly on the barrier and the solver handles the discontinuous data smoothly, rather than letting the barrier fall between nodes. price_barrier starts the grid at the barrier, and the result matches the method of images, C_{do}(S) = C(S) - (H/S)^{2\lambda - 2} C(H^2/S).">
