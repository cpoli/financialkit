Examples
========

This gallery walks through every public feature of
``financialkit.pde_methods``: the explicit, implicit and Crank-Nicolson
schemes, their stability and accuracy, American options as linear
complementarity problems, and a two-asset ADI solver. Each script
illustrates one dated breakthrough from the
:doc:`history page </history/pde_methods_breakthroughs>`.

See also the narrative tutorial:

- :doc:`/tutorials/one_option_five_pricers`

Each script in this gallery is self-contained and can be run directly with
``python examples/pde_methods/<section>/<script>.py``.

Sections
--------

- **schemes** -- Crank-Nicolson, Laasonen's implicit scheme, the Thomas
  algorithm, the heat-equation transform, Schwartz's explicit scheme,
  the explicit scheme as a trinomial tree, and barrier options.
- **stability** -- the Courant-Friedrichs-Lewy condition, von Neumann
  analysis and Rannacher smoothing.
- **accuracy** -- Richardson extrapolation and Tavella-Randall grids.
- **american** -- Cryer's projected SOR, the Brennan-Schwartz algorithm
  and the penalty method.
- **multi_factor** -- the Peaceman-Rachford ADI scheme for two assets.

American options
----------------

Early exercise turns each implicit step into a linear complementarity
problem: projected SOR, the Brennan-Schwartz direct solve and the
penalty method.


.. raw:: html

  <div id='sg-tag-list' class='sphx-glr-tag-list'></div>


.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbnails">

.. thumbnail-parent-div-open

.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="An American option is either held, where it satisfies the Black-Scholes equation, or exercised, where it equals its payoff; each implicit step becomes a linear complementarity problem Ax \ge b,\ x \ge g,\ (Ax - b)^\top(x - g) = 0. Colin Cryer (1971) solved it by successive over-relaxation, projecting each update back onto x \ge g. psor does this inside CrankNicolson; the sweep count depends strongly on the relaxation parameter \omega.">

  .. image:: /api/gallery/pde_methods/american/images/thumb/sphx_glr_plot_01_cryer_psor_thumb.png

.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Michael Brennan and Eduardo Schwartz (1977) priced the American put with an implicit scheme and a direct solve: eliminate the tridiagonal system starting from the top of the grid, then substitute back upward from S = 0, taking the larger of the computed value and the payoff at each node. Because the put is exercised on a single interval at the bottom of the grid, this one pass solves the complementarity problem exactly. brennan_schwartz matches PSOR to rounding at a fraction of the cost.">

  .. image:: /api/gallery/pde_methods/american/images/thumb/sphx_glr_plot_02_brennan_schwartz_thumb.png

.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Peter Forsyth and Kenneth Vetzal (2002) replaced the complementarity constraint by a penalty: on nodes where the value falls below the payoff, add \rho\,(g - x) with a large \rho. The resulting nonlinear system is solved by a Newton (policy) iteration that only changes which nodes are penalised, and converges in two or three tridiagonal solves per time step, independently of the grid size -- unlike PSOR, whose sweep count grows as the grid is refined. penalty_solve implements it.">

Schemes
-------

The Black-Scholes equation on a grid: explicit, implicit and
Crank-Nicolson time stepping, the tridiagonal solves they need, the
transformation to the heat equation, the link to trinomial trees, and
knock-out barriers.

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Pentti Laasonen (1949) evaluated the space derivatives of the heat equation at the new time level, (I - \Delta\tau A) V^{n+1} = V^n. Every step is then a linear solve, but no step is too large: the scheme is stable and monotone for any \Delta\tau, at the price of first-order accuracy in time. ImplicitScheme shows both sides: a large step that would wreck the explicit scheme gives a sensible answer, and halving the step halves the error.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Every implicit step in one space dimension is a tridiagonal system. Llewellyn Thomas (1949) wrote down the specialised Gaussian elimination that solves it in O(m) operations: one sweep removes the sub-diagonal, a second substitutes back. No pivoting is needed because the implicit Black-Scholes matrix is diagonally dominant. thomas_solve is that algorithm by hand; it agrees with scipy.linalg.solve_banded to rounding, and its cost grows linearly while a dense solve grows cubically.">

Stability
---------

When a scheme's errors grow without bound: the CFL time-step limit of the
explicit scheme, von Neumann's Fourier analysis, and Rannacher's cure for
Crank-Nicolson's undamped oscillations.


.. raw:: html

  <div id='sg-tag-list' class='sphx-glr-tag-list'></div>


.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbnails">

.. thumbnail-parent-div-open

.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Courant, Friedrichs and Lewy (1928) proved that an explicit difference scheme cannot converge if its time step outruns the way information spreads across the grid. For the diffusion in the Black-Scholes equation the bound ties the time step to the square of the spot step: \Delta t \lesssim 1/(\sigma^2 M^2). explicit_stability computes the limit from the eigenvalues of the discrete operator; a step 3% below it gives the right price, a step 30% above it blows up from rounding errors near the top of the grid.">

.. only:: html

  .. image:: /api/gallery/pde_methods/stability/images/thumb/sphx_glr_plot_01_cfl_explicit_blowup_thumb.png
    :alt:

  :doc:`/api/gallery/pde_methods/stability/plot_01_cfl_explicit_blowup`

.. raw:: html

      <div class="sphx-glr-thumbnail-title">Courant-Friedrichs-Lewy</div>
    </div>


.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="John von Neumann&#x27;s method, used at Los Alamos in the 1940s and published in 1950, inserts a single Fourier mode V_j^n = g^n e^{ij\xi} into a constant-coefficient scheme. The mode grows when its amplification factor has |g(\xi)| &gt; 1, so stability means |g| \le 1 for every \xi. For the theta-scheme in log-spot, g = (1 + (1-\theta)z)/(1 - \theta z) with z \approx -\sigma^2\Delta t/\Delta x^2 (1 - \cos\xi): von_neumann_amplification shows the explicit scheme failing above a diffusion number of one, Crank-Nicolson never growing but barely damping the highest modes, and the implicit scheme damping them all.">

  .. image:: /api/gallery/pde_methods/stability/images/thumb/sphx_glr_plot_02_von_neumann_thumb.png

.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Crank-Nicolson is stable, but its amplification factor tends to -1 for the stiffest modes, so the high-frequency error excited by a payoff kink is never damped: it just flips sign every step. With large time steps the price looks fine while delta and gamma oscillate around the strike. Rolf Rannacher (1984) proposed starting with a few fully implicit steps, which damp those modes, before switching to Crank-Nicolson. CrankNicolson with rannacher_steps=2 replaces the first two steps by four implicit half steps.">
