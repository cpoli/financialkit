The stochastic discount factor
------------------------------

No arbitrage as the existence of positive state prices, and the
volatility a discount factor needs to price the assets.

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Michael Harrison and David Kreps (1979) proved the fundamental theorem of asset pricing: a market has no arbitrage if and only if some strictly positive linear rule prices every traded payoff. With finitely many states it is a vector of state prices q_s &gt; 0 with p = X&#x27;q; divided by the physical probabilities it is the stochastic discount factor m_s = q_s / \pi_s, so that p = E[m x]; normalised, it is the risk-neutral measure. In a complete market the state prices are unique. In an incomplete one, a new claim has an interval of arbitrage-free prices.">
