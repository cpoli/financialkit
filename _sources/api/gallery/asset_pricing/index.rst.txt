Examples
========

This gallery walks through every public feature of
``financialkit.asset_pricing``: equity valuation by discounted
dividends, Modigliani-Miller and Shiller's variance bound; the CAPM,
Jensen's alpha, the zero-beta CAPM and the GRS test; factor regressions,
Fama-MacBeth, the APT, Fama-French and Carhart factors; consumption-based
pricing and the equity premium puzzle; and the stochastic discount
factor.

Each script in this gallery is self-contained and can be run directly with
``python examples/asset_pricing/<section>/<script>.py``.

Sections
--------

- **valuation** -- Williams's dividend discount model, Modigliani-Miller,
  Gordon growth and Shiller's excess volatility.
- **capm** -- the security market line, Jensen's alpha, Black's zero-beta
  CAPM and the Gibbons-Ross-Shanken test.
- **factors** -- Fama-MacBeth regressions, Ross's APT, the Fama-French
  three factors and Carhart's momentum.
- **consumption** -- Lucas's tree, Breeden's consumption CAPM and the
  equity premium puzzle.
- **sdf** -- Harrison-Kreps state prices and the Hansen-Jagannathan bound.

The stochastic discount factor
------------------------------

No arbitrage as the existence of positive state prices, and the
volatility a discount factor needs to price the assets.

.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Michael Harrison and David Kreps (1979) proved the fundamental theorem of asset pricing: a market has no arbitrage if and only if some strictly positive linear rule prices every traded payoff. With finitely many states it is a vector of state prices q_s &gt; 0 with p = X&#x27;q; divided by the physical probabilities it is the stochastic discount factor m_s = q_s / \pi_s, so that p = E[m x]; normalised, it is the risk-neutral measure. In a complete market the state prices are unique. In an incomplete one, a new claim has an interval of arbitrage-free prices.">
