Examples
========

This gallery walks through every public feature of
``financialkit.market_microstructure``: spread measures and estimators,
trade classification, dealer and market-making models, asymmetric
information and informed-trading measures, optimal execution and
illiquidity.

Each script in this gallery is self-contained and can be run directly with
``python examples/market_microstructure/<section>/<script>.py``.

Sections
--------

- **spreads** -- Demsetz's spreads, Roll's estimator, Lee-Ready trade
  classification and the Corwin-Schultz high-low estimator.
- **dealers** -- Garman's Poisson dealer, Ho-Stoll inventory quotes and
  Avellaneda-Stoikov market making.
- **information** -- Kyle's insider, Glosten-Milgrom, Hasbrouck's
  trade-impact VAR, PIN and VPIN.
- **execution** -- the square-root impact law, Bertsimas-Lo and
  Almgren-Chriss.
- **liquidity** -- Amihud's illiquidity ratio.

.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Mark Garman (1976) started market microstructure by modelling orders as Poisson streams: buyers arrive at rate \lambda_a(a), falling with the ask, and sellers at \lambda_b(b), rising with the bid. A monopolist dealer holding stock and cash fails when either runs out. The stock follows a gambler&#x27;s-ruin walk, so the probability of eventually running out of shares is (\lambda_a/\lambda_b)^{I_0} when sellers outnumber buyers, and one otherwise. Keeping both stock and cash growing requires \lambda_b &gt; \lambda_a and a\lambda_a &gt; b\lambda_b, hence a &gt; b: the dealer must set a spread.">

Holding inventory exposes it to price moves, so a dealer who is long lowers both quotes, making a sale to it less attractive and a purchase from it more so, until the inventory returns to zero. With order arrivals \lambda = \alpha - \beta\delta on each side, the spread itself, \alpha/\beta, does not depend on the inventory: only its position does.">

A trader with exponential utility first computes the reservation price r = s - q\gamma\sigma^2(T - t), the mid price adjusted for its inventory q, and then quotes a spread \gamma\sigma^2(T-t) + \frac{2}{\gamma}\ln(1 + \gamma/k) around it. A competitive market maker sees only the total flow y = x + u and sets p = E[v \mid y] = p_0 + \lambda y. In equilibrium">

.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Lawrence Glosten and Paul Milgrom (1985) explained the spread with no inventory and no costs. A competitive dealer trades one unit at a time with traders who are informed with probability \alpha. Since a buy is more likely to come from someone who knows the value is high, the dealer sets the ask to E[V \mid \text{buy}] and the bid to E[V \mid \text{sell}], losing to the informed exactly what it gains from the uninformed. Each trade updates the dealer&#x27;s belief by Bayes&#x27; rule, so prices are a martingale that converges to the truth.">

.. only:: html

  .. image:: /api/gallery/market_microstructure/information/images/thumb/sphx_glr_plot_02_glosten_milgrom_thumb.png
    :alt:

  :doc:`/api/gallery/market_microstructure/information/plot_02_glosten_milgrom`

.. raw:: html

      <div class="sphx-glr-thumbnail-title">Glosten and Milgrom: a spread from adverse selection alone</div>
    </div>


.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Joel Hasbrouck (1991) measured the information in trades with a vector autoregression of quote revisions r_t and signed trades x_t. Order flow is persistent and quotes also react to inventory, so a trade&#x27;s immediate price effect overstates what it reveals. The cumulative impulse response of the quote to an unexpected trade separates the two: it settles at the permanent impact, the information content, after the transitory part has decayed.">

.. only:: html

  .. image:: /api/gallery/market_microstructure/information/images/thumb/sphx_glr_plot_03_hasbrouck_var_thumb.png

.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Easley, Kiefer, O&#x27;Hara and Paperman (1996) turned the Glosten-Milgrom logic into an estimator that needs only daily counts of buys and sells. On a day with private news (probability \alpha), informed traders add rate \mu to one side; otherwise only uninformed buys and sells arrive, at rates \varepsilon_b, \varepsilon_s. The fitted mixture gives the share of trades that are informed, \mathrm{PIN} = \alpha\mu / (\alpha\mu + \varepsilon_b + \varepsilon_s). Their finding: thinly traded stocks have a higher PIN, which explains their wider spreads.">

Easley, López de Prado and O&#x27;Hara (2012) measured order imbalance in volume time instead: cut the trading record into buckets of equal volume, split each one-minute bar&#x27;s volume into buys and sells by bulk volume classification, V^B = V\Phi(\Delta P/\sigma_{\Delta P}), and average |V^B - V^S|/V over the last 50 buckets. Their VPIN rose for hours before the flash crash of 6 May 2010.">

The price of immediacy, measured from quotes, from trades, and from prices alone.

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Harold Demsetz (1968) argued that a trader who wants to trade now pays the dealer the spread for standing ready, and that the spread shrinks as trading activity grows, because a busy dealer turns over inventory faster. Today the spread is measured three ways: the quoted spread on the screen, the effective spread actually paid relative to the midquote, and the realized spread the liquidity provider keeps once the price has moved; the difference is the price impact, lost to better-informed traders.">
