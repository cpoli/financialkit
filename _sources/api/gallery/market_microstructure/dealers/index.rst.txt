Dealers
-------

Market makers who manage order flow, inventory and risk.

.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Mark Garman (1976) started market microstructure by modelling orders as Poisson streams: buyers arrive at rate \lambda_a(a), falling with the ask, and sellers at \lambda_b(b), rising with the bid. A monopolist dealer holding stock and cash fails when either runs out. The stock follows a gambler&#x27;s-ruin walk, so the probability of eventually running out of shares is (\lambda_a/\lambda_b)^{I_0} when sellers outnumber buyers, and one otherwise. Keeping both stock and cash growing requires \lambda_b &gt; \lambda_a and a\lambda_a &gt; b\lambda_b, hence a &gt; b: the dealer must set a spread.">

Holding inventory exposes it to price moves, so a dealer who is long lowers both quotes, making a sale to it less attractive and a purchase from it more so, until the inventory returns to zero. With order arrivals \lambda = \alpha - \beta\delta on each side, the spread itself, \alpha/\beta, does not depend on the inventory: only its position does.">

A trader with exponential utility first computes the reservation price r = s - q\gamma\sigma^2(T - t), the mid price adjusted for its inventory q, and then quotes a spread \gamma\sigma^2(T-t) + \frac{2}{\gamma}\ln(1 + \gamma/k) around it. The example reproduces the comparison of their Table 1: the inventory strategy gives up a little expected profit for a much smaller variance.">
