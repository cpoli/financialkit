    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Frank Redington (1952), an actuary, asked how a life office could hold assets so that a change in interest rates leaves its surplus unharmed. His answer has three conditions: match the present values, match the durations, and make the assets more convex than the liabilities. Then the surplus has a local minimum at today&#x27;s rates. Assets that straddle the liabilities satisfy the third condition. Assets bunched inside them do not.">
