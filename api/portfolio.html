financialkit.portfolio hub · History · Examples · API reference

Markowitz’s efficient frontier, closed form and long-only through scipy.optimize.minimize; Merton’s analytic constants; Tobin’s tangency portfolio and two-fund separation; the Kelly criterion, Merton’s portfolio problem (wealth simulated through financialkit.integrators.gbm_paths()) and CPPI; Sharpe’s single-index model and Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage; Black-Litterman and Michaud resampling; Rockafellar-Uryasev CVaR optimization (scipy.optimize.linprog), the most-diversified portfolio, risk parity and hierarchical risk parity (scipy.cluster.hierarchy); the Sharpe, Sortino and information ratios, Grinold’s fundamental law and rolling out-of-sample tests against 1/N.

financialkit.portfolio: mean-variance optimization, growth-optimal strategies, covariance estimation and risk-based allocation.

Markowitz’s efficient frontier (closed form and long-only through scipy.optimize.minimize()), Merton’s analytic constants, Tobin’s tangency portfolio and two-fund separation; Kelly’s criterion, Merton’s portfolio problem and CPPI; Sharpe’s single-index model and Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage; Black-Litterman and Michaud resampling; Rockafellar-Uryasev CVaR optimization (scipy.optimize.linprog()), the most-diversified portfolio, risk parity and hierarchical risk parity; and the Sharpe, Sortino and information ratios, Grinold’s fundamental law and rolling out-of-sample tests against 1/N. Returns are simple and per period, weights are fractions of wealth.

financialkit.portfolio.cppi(prices, times, floor=0.8, multiplier=3.0, r=0.0, max_leverage=inf, w0=1.0)[source]#

Constant proportion portfolio insurance rebalanced on a discrete grid.

At each date the strategy holds \(E = \min(m\,\max(W - F, 0), \ell W)\) in the risky asset and the rest at the riskless rate, where the floor \(F_t = F_0 e^{rt}\) grows like a zero-coupon bond. Between dates the risky asset can fall by more than \(1/m\), and wealth then drops through the floor: that is gap risk.

Parameters:

  • prices (ArrayLike) – Risky asset paths, e.g. from financialkit.integrators.gbm_paths().

  • times (ArrayLike)

  • floor (float) – Initial floor as a fraction of w0.

  • multiplier (float) – Exposure per unit of cushion, \(m\).

  • r (float) – Riskless rate.

  • max_leverage (float) – Cap \(\ell\) on exposure as a multiple of wealth (1 forbids borrowing).

  • w0 (float) – Initial wealth.

Return type:

CPPIResult

Returns:

CPPIResult

Examples

>>> import numpy as np
>>> res = cppi([[100.0, 90.0, 99.0]], [0.0, 0.5, 1.0], floor=0.8, multiplier=2.0)
>>> np.round(res.wealth, 4)
array([[1.   , 0.96 , 0.992]])
financialkit.portfolio.cppi_continuous(prices, times, sigma, floor=0.8, multiplier=3.0, r=0.0, w0=1.0)[source]#

Black and Perold’s closed form for CPPI under continuous rebalancing.

For geometric Brownian motion the cushion is a power of the price, \(C_t = C_0 (S_t/S_0)^m \exp\left((1 - m)(r + \tfrac12 m\sigma^2)t\right)\), so wealth \(F_t + C_t\) never falls below the floor.

Parameters:

  • times (ArrayLike)

  • sigma (float) – Volatility of the risky asset.

  • multiplier (float) – As in cppi() (no leverage cap).

  • w0 (float) – As in cppi() (no leverage cap).

financialkit.portfolio.hierarchical_risk_parity(cov, method='single')[source]#

Lopez de Prado’s hierarchical risk parity.

  1. Cluster the assets with scipy.cluster.hierarchy.linkage() on the distance \(\sqrt{(1 - \rho_{ij})/2}\).

  2. Reorder them by the dendrogram’s leaves (quasi-diagonalization).

  3. Bisect the ordered list recursively, splitting each parent’s weight between its halves in inverse proportion to the variance of each half’s inverse-variance portfolio.

Parameters:

  • cov (ArrayLike)

  • method (str) – Linkage method ("single" in the original paper).

Return type:

HRPResult

Returns:

HRPResult

Examples

With a diagonal covariance, HRP reduces to inverse-variance weights.

>>> res = hierarchical_risk_parity(np.diag([0.01, 0.04, 0.04, 0.16]))
>>> np.round(res.weights * 25, 6)
array([16.,  4.,  4.,  1.])

financialkit.portfolio.simulate_active_management(ic, breadth, n_periods, rng=None)[source]#

Simulate a manager with skill ic making breadth independent bets a period.
