financialkit.pde_methods#

financialkit.pde_methods hub · History · Examples · API reference

Finite-difference methods for the Black-Scholes equation: the theta-method family (Schwartz’s explicit scheme with its stability check, Laasonen’s implicit scheme and Crank-Nicolson with Rannacher’s implicit start) on uniform or Tavella-Randall spot grids, with tridiagonal systems solved by scipy.linalg.solve_banded() or the Thomas algorithm; knock-out barriers on a barrier-aligned grid; American options as linear complementarity problems solved by projected SOR, the Brennan-Schwartz algorithm or the penalty method; von Neumann amplification factors, the explicit log-spot scheme as a trinomial tree, the transformation to the heat equation and Richardson extrapolation; and the Peaceman-Rachford ADI scheme for two-asset options.

financialkit.pde_methods: finite-difference methods for option pricing.

The Black-Scholes equation on a spot grid by the theta-method: Schwartz’s explicit scheme with its stability check, Laasonen’s implicit scheme and Crank-Nicolson with Rannacher’s implicit start, the tridiagonal systems solved by scipy.linalg.solve_banded() or a hand-written Thomas algorithm, uniform and Tavella-Randall grids, and knock-out barriers on a barrier-aligned grid. American options by Cryer’s projected SOR, the Brennan-Schwartz direct solve and Forsyth-Vetzal’s penalty iteration. The theory around the schemes: von Neumann amplification factors, the explicit log-spot scheme as a trinomial tree, the transformation to the heat equation and Richardson extrapolation. Two assets by the Peaceman-Rachford ADI scheme.

class financialkit.pde_methods.CrankNicolson(n_space=200, n_time=200, rannacher_steps=0, **kwargs)[source]#

Bases: ThetaScheme

The Crank-Nicolson scheme (theta = 1/2), optionally with Rannacher’s start.

Second-order accurate in time and unconditionally stable, but its amplification factor tends to -1 for the stiffest modes, so a payoff kink leaves undamped oscillations in delta and gamma when the time step is large; rannacher_steps=2 removes them.

Parameters:

  • n_space (int) – Spot intervals and time steps.

  • n_time (int) – Spot intervals and time steps.

  • rannacher_steps (int) – Initial steps replaced by two implicit half steps each.

  • **kwargs – Any other ThetaScheme option except theta.

Examples

>>> res = CrankNicolson(400, 100, rannacher_steps=2).price(100.0, 100.0, 1.0, 0.05, 0.2)
>>> round(res.price, 2)
10.45

financialkit.pde_methods.explicit_stability(S, dt, sigma, r, q=0.0)[source]#

Check an explicit step against the largest stable step on a spot grid.

The explicit step multiplies the interior values by \(I + \Delta t A\), with \(A\) the discrete Black-Scholes operator, so errors stay bounded only while \(|1 + \Delta t \lambda| \le 1\) for every eigenvalue \(\lambda\) of \(A\). The limit returned is the largest such step, \(\min_\lambda -2\operatorname{Re}\lambda / |\lambda|^2\); on a uniform grid from 0 it is close to \(1/(\sigma^2 (M - 1)^2)\), the CFL-type bound that ties the time step to the square of the spot step.

Parameters:

  • S (ArrayLike) – Spot grid.

  • dt (float) – Time step.

  • sigma (float)

  • r (float)

  • q (float)

Return type:

StabilityResult

Returns:

StabilityResult

Examples

>>> res = explicit_stability(np.linspace(0.0, 400.0, 101), 0.002, 0.2, 0.05)
>>> round(res.limit, 5), res.stable
(0.00279, True)

financialkit.pde_methods.penalty_solve(lower, diag, upper, rhs, obstacle, x0=None, penalty=100000000.0, tol=1e-10, max_iter=50)[source]#

Forsyth and Vetzal’s penalty iteration for a tridiagonal LCP.

Iterates \((A + P^k) x^{k+1} = b + P^k g\), where \(P^k\) is diagonal with \(\rho\) on the nodes where \(x^k < g\) and 0 elsewhere, until the set of penalised nodes stops changing. The solution satisfies the constraint to within \(O(1/\rho)\).

Parameters:

  • lower (ArrayLike) – Tridiagonal matrix (lower[0] and upper[-1] ignored).

  • diag (ArrayLike) – Tridiagonal matrix (lower[0] and upper[-1] ignored).

  • upper (ArrayLike) – Tridiagonal matrix (lower[0] and upper[-1] ignored).

  • rhs (ArrayLike)

  • obstacle (ArrayLike)

  • x0 (ArrayLike | None) – Starting point (default: the unconstrained solution).

  • penalty (float) – Penalty factor \(\rho\).

  • tol (float) – Relative change below which the iteration stops.

  • max_iter (int)

Return type:

LCPSolution

Returns:

LCPSolution

Examples

>>> sol = penalty_solve([0, -1, -1], [2, 2, 2], [-1, -1, 0], [1, 0, 1], [1.5, 0, 0])
