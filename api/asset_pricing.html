financialkit.asset_pricing#

financialkit.asset_pricing hub · History · Examples · API reference

Williams’s dividend discount model and Gordon growth (through financialkit.time_value.perpetuity_present_value()), Modigliani-Miller and the weighted average cost of capital, and Shiller’s variance bound; Sharpe’s CAPM with the market as the tangency portfolio (financialkit.risk.safety_first_portfolio()), Jensen’s alpha, Black’s zero-beta CAPM and the Gibbons-Ross-Shanken test; time-series factor regressions (through financialkit.time_series.ols()), Fama-MacBeth premia, Ross’s APT and its arbitrage portfolios, and the characteristic sorts behind the Fama-French and Carhart factors; Lucas’s tree, Breeden’s consumption CAPM and the Mehra-Prescott economy; Harrison-Kreps state prices with scipy.optimize.linprog and the Hansen-Jagannathan bound.

financialkit.asset_pricing: valuation, the CAPM, factor models, consumption-based pricing and the SDF.

Williams’s dividend discount model and Gordon growth (through financialkit.time_value.perpetuity_present_value()), Modigliani-Miller and the cost of capital, Shiller’s variance bound; Sharpe’s CAPM with the market as the tangency portfolio, Jensen’s alpha, Black’s zero-beta CAPM and the Gibbons-Ross-Shanken test; time-series factor regressions (through financialkit.time_series.ols()), Fama-MacBeth premia, Ross’s APT and arbitrage portfolios, and the characteristic sorts behind Fama-French and Carhart factors; Lucas’s tree, Breeden’s consumption CAPM and Mehra-Prescott’s equity premium puzzle; Harrison-Kreps state prices and the Hansen-Jagannathan bound. Return panels have shape (n_periods, n_assets), and the consumption-based models use gross returns.

class financialkit.asset_pricing.LucasTree(beta, gamma, mu=0.018, sigma=0.036)[source]#

Bases: ConsumptionEconomy

Lucas’s tree with i.i.d. lognormal growth: every price in closed form.

Log consumption growth is \(g \sim N(\mu, \sigma^2)\), and the tree’s dividend is consumption. The price-dividend ratio is constant, \(P/D = \kappa / (1 - \kappa)\) with \(\kappa = \beta e^{(1-\gamma)\mu + (1-\gamma)^2\sigma^2/2}\), the riskless rate is \(\log R_f = -\log\beta + \gamma\mu - \gamma^2\sigma^2/2\), and the log equity premium is \(\log E[R_e] - \log R_f = \gamma\sigma^2\).

Parameters:

  • beta (float) – Time-discount factor.

  • gamma (float) – Relative risk aversion.

  • mu (float) – Mean of log consumption growth per period.

  • sigma (float) – Volatility of log consumption growth.

Examples

>>> m = LucasTree(beta=0.99, gamma=2.0, mu=0.02, sigma=0.036).moments()
>>> round(m.risk_free - 1, 4), round(m.equity_premium, 4)
(0.0486, 0.0027)

class financialkit.asset_pricing.MehraPrescott(beta, gamma, mu=0.018, delta=0.036, phi=0.43)[source]#

Bases: ConsumptionEconomy

Mehra and Prescott’s two-state Markov economy.

Gross consumption growth is \(\lambda_1 = 1 + \mu + \delta\) or \(\lambda_2 = 1 + \mu - \delta\), and the chain stays in its state with probability \(\phi\). The price-dividend ratios \(w_i\) solve \(w_i = \beta \sum_j \phi_{ij} \lambda_j^{1-\gamma}(w_j + 1)\), and the riskless return in state \(i\) is \(1 / (\beta \sum_j \phi_{ij} \lambda_j^{-\gamma})\). The defaults are their 1889-1978 calibration: mean growth 1.8%, standard deviation 3.6% and first-order autocorrelation -0.14.

Parameters:

  • beta (float)

  • gamma (float)

  • mu (float) – Mean consumption growth.

  • delta (float) – Half the gap between the two growth states (their standard deviation).

  • phi (float) – Probability of staying in the current state; the autocorrelation is 2 phi - 1.

Examples

>>> m = MehraPrescott(beta=0.99, gamma=10.0).moments()
>>> round(m.risk_free - 1, 3), round(m.equity_premium, 4)
(0.131, 0.0269)

financialkit.asset_pricing.apt_arbitrage_portfolio(expected_returns, betas)[source]#

A zero-cost, zero-beta portfolio that earns any deviation from linear pricing.

The weights are the residuals of the cross-sectional regression of expected returns on a constant and the betas. Being orthogonal to the constant, they sum to zero (no cost); being orthogonal to every beta, they carry no factor risk; and their expected return is the sum of squared residuals, positive unless the APT holds exactly. With many assets the idiosyncratic risk of this portfolio diversifies away, which is Ross’s argument that the residuals must be small.

Parameters:

  • expected_returns (ArrayLike)

  • betas (ArrayLike)

Return type:

ndarray

Returns:

ndarray, shape (n,) – Weights; their expected return is weights @ expected_returns.

Examples

>>> import numpy as np
>>> w = apt_arbitrage_portfolio([0.05, 0.08, 0.13], [0.0, 1.0, 2.0])
>>> bool(np.isclose(w.sum(), 0.0, atol=1e-9)), bool(np.isclose(w @ [0.0, 1.0, 2.0], 0.0, atol=1e-9))
(True, True)
>>> bool(np.isclose(w @ [0.05, 0.08, 0.13], 6.6667e-05, rtol=1e-4))
True

financialkit.asset_pricing.fama_macbeth(excess_returns, factors=None, betas=None)[source]#

Fama and MacBeth’s two-pass estimate of factor risk premia.

First pass: each asset’s betas, from full-sample time-series regressions on factors (or supplied directly as betas). Second pass: each period, a cross-sectional regression of the excess returns on a constant and the betas. The premia are the time averages of the slopes, with standard errors from their time-series variation, which absorbs cross-sectional correlation of the errors.

Parameters:

  • excess_returns (ArrayLike)

  • factors (ArrayLike | None)

  • betas (ArrayLike | None) – Exactly one of factors and betas must be given.

Return type:

FamaMacBethResult

Returns:

FamaMacBethResult – premia[0] is the average zero-beta excess return (zero if the model holds), premia[1:] the factor premia.

Gibbons, Ross and Shanken’s F-test that all time-series alphas are zero.

\[F = \frac{T - N - K}{N}\, \frac{\hat\alpha' \hat\Sigma^{-1} \hat\alpha}{1 + \bar f' \hat\Omega^{-1} \bar f} \sim F_{N,\, T - N - K}\]

with \(\hat\Sigma\) and \(\hat\Omega\) the maximum-likelihood residual and factor covariances. The statistic measures how much the squared Sharpe ratio of the tangency portfolio improves when the test assets are added to the factors.

Parameters:

  • excess_returns (ArrayLike) – Test assets, with n < T - k.

  • factors (ArrayLike) – Traded factor excess returns.

Return type:

GRSResult

Returns:

GRSResult

financialkit.asset_pricing.hansen_jagannathan_bound(mean_returns, cov, sdf_means)[source]#

The smallest volatility of a discount factor that prices a set of gross returns.

For \(E[m R] = 1\) with \(E[m] = v\), \(\sigma^2(m) \ge (\mathbf 1 - v \mu)' \Sigma^{-1} (\mathbf 1 - v \mu)\), attained by \(m^* = v + (\mathbf 1 - v\mu)'\Sigma^{-1}(R - \mu)\).
