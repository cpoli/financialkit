financialkit.exotic_options#

financialkit.exotic_options hub · History · Examples · API reference

Barrier options: Merton’s down-and-out call, the Reiner-Rubinstein closed forms for all eight single barriers with rebates, Ikeda-Kunitomo double barriers, the Broadie-Glasserman-Kou correction for discrete monitoring and Monte Carlo with Brownian-bridge crossing probabilities; binary options (cash-or-nothing, asset-or-nothing, gap and one-touch); Asian options (Kemna-Vorst geometric closed forms, arithmetic Monte Carlo with the geometric control variate, Turnbull-Wakeman moment matching); floating and fixed lookbacks with bridge-sampled extrema; variance swaps replicated by a strip of options; Geske compound options, forward starts and choosers; Margrabe exchange options, Stulz options on the maximum and minimum and Reiner quantos; and American options by Longstaff-Schwartz least-squares Monte Carlo. Paths come from financialkit.integrators and Monte Carlo results from financialkit.monte_carlo.

financialkit.exotic_options: barrier, binary, Asian, lookback, compound, multi-asset and American options.

Barriers: Merton’s down-and-out call by the method of images, the Reiner-Rubinstein closed forms for all eight single-barrier options with rebates, Ikeda-Kunitomo double barriers, the Broadie-Glasserman-Kou correction for discrete monitoring, and Monte Carlo with Brownian-bridge crossing probabilities. Binaries: cash-or-nothing, asset-or-nothing, gap and one-touch options. Asians: Kemna-Vorst geometric closed forms, the arithmetic average by Monte Carlo with the geometric control variate, and Turnbull-Wakeman moment matching. Lookbacks: Goldman-Sosin-Gatto floating and Conze-Viswanathan fixed strikes, with exact bridge-sampled extrema for Monte Carlo. Variance swaps replicated by a strip of out-of-the-money options (Demeterfi-Derman-Kamal-Zou). Geske compound options, Rubinstein forward starts and choosers, Margrabe exchange options, Stulz options on the maximum or minimum of two assets, Reiner quantos, and American options by Longstaff-Schwartz least-squares Monte Carlo. Paths are simulated through financialkit.integrators, and Monte Carlo results are financialkit.monte_carlo result objects.

financialkit.exotic_options.barrier_crossing_probability(x0, x1, b, sigma, dt)[source]#

Probability that a Brownian bridge from x0 to x1 crosses the level b.

For a log-price with volatility \(\sigma\) over a step \(\Delta t\), with both endpoints on the same side of \(b\),

\[P = \exp\!\left(-\frac{2 (x_0 - b)(x_1 - b)}{\sigma^2 \Delta t}\right);\]

it is 1 if either endpoint is on the far side. The drift does not matter once both endpoints are fixed (Glasserman 2004, Sec. 6.4).

financialkit.exotic_options.barrier_price(S, K, H, T, r, sigma, q=0.0, option_type='call', barrier_type='down-and-out', rebate=0.0)[source]#

Reiner-Rubinstein (1991) price of a continuously monitored single-barrier option.

With \(b = r - q\), \(\mu = (b - \sigma^2/2)/\sigma^2\) and \(\lambda = \sqrt{\mu^2 + 2r/\sigma^2}\), every one of the eight calls and puts is a sum of the building blocks \(A, \dots, F\) of Haug (2007, Sec. 4.17.1): \(A\) is the vanilla option, \(B\) the vanilla struck at the barrier, \(C\) and \(D\) their reflections through the barrier, \(E\) a knock-in’s rebate paid at expiry if the barrier is never hit and \(F\) a knock-out’s rebate paid when it is hit. For example, for \(K > H\),

\[C_{do} = A - C + F, \qquad C_{di} = C + E.\]

In-out parity holds when the rebate is zero: knock-in + knock-out = vanilla. If the spot is already through the barrier, a knock-out is worth its rebate and a knock-in the vanilla option.

Parameters:

  • K (ArrayLike) – Spot, strike, barrier, maturity, rate and volatility.

  • H (ArrayLike) – Spot, strike, barrier, maturity, rate and volatility.

  • T (ArrayLike) – Spot, strike, barrier, maturity, rate and volatility.

  • r (ArrayLike) – Spot, strike, barrier, maturity, rate and volatility.

  • sigma (ArrayLike) – Spot, strike, barrier, maturity, rate and volatility.

  • q (ArrayLike) – Continuous dividend yield.

  • option_type (str)

  • barrier_type (str)

  • rebate (ArrayLike) – Cash paid when a knock-out is knocked out, or at expiry when a knock-in never knocks in.

Return type:

ndarray | float

Returns:

float or ndarray

Examples

financialkit.exotic_options.bivariate_normal_cdf(h, k, rho)[source]#

Standard bivariate normal distribution \(M(h, k; \rho) = P(X \le h, Y \le k)\).

Computed exactly from Owen’s T function (scipy.special.owens_t()):

\[M(h, k; \rho) = \tfrac12 N(h) + \tfrac12 N(k) - T\!\left(h, \frac{k - \rho h}{h \sqrt{1 - \rho^2}}\right) - T\!\left(k, \frac{h - \rho k}{k \sqrt{1 - \rho^2}}\right) - \beta,\]

with \(\beta = 0\) when \(h\) and \(k\) have the same sign and \(\tfrac12\) otherwise (Owen 1956). It broadcasts over its inputs, which the compound and rainbow option formulas rely on.

financialkit.exotic_options.bridge_extremum(log_paths, sigma, dt, kind='max', rng=None)[source]#

Exact samples of each path’s continuous maximum or minimum given its grid values.

Within a step from \(x_0\) to \(x_1\) the maximum of the Brownian bridge is

\[M = \tfrac12\left[x_0 + x_1 + \sqrt{(x_1 - x_0)^2 - 2\sigma^2 \Delta t \ln U}\right], \quad U \sim U(0, 1],\]

and the minimum is the mirror image (Glasserman 2004, Sec. 6.4). The path’s extremum is the extremum over its steps.

  • sigma (float) – Volatility.

  • dt (float) – Step length in years.

  • kind (str)

  • rng (numpy.random.Generator or int, optional) – Generator or seed.

Return type:

ndarray

Returns:

ndarray, shape (n_paths,) – The log of each path’s running maximum (or minimum) over the whole interval.

Geske (1979) compound option: an option expiring at \(T_1\) with strike \(K_1\) on an option to \(T_2\) struck at \(K_2\).

With \(I\) the critical spot (critical_price()), \(\rho = \sqrt{T_1/T_2}\), \(y_{1,2}\) the Black-Scholes \(d_{1,2}\) for strike \(I\) and horizon \(T_1\), and \(z_{1,2}\) those for \(K_2\) and \(T_2\), the call on a call is

\[S e^{-q T_2} M(z_1, y_1; \rho) - K_2 e^{-r T_2} M(z_2, y_2; \rho) - K_1 e^{-r T_1} N(y_2),\]

financialkit.exotic_options.double_barrier_price(S, K, L, U, T, r, sigma, q=0.0, option_type='call', knock='out', n_terms=5)[source]#

Ikeda-Kunitomo (1992) double-barrier option with flat barriers \(L < S < U\).

The knock-out call is a series of images reflected alternately through both barriers (Haug 2007, Sec. 4.17.3, with zero curvature):

\[c = S e^{(b - r)T} \sum_{n=-N}^{N} \left[\left(\tfrac{U^n}{L^n}\right)^{\mu} (N(d_1) - N(d_2)) - \left(\tfrac{L^{n+1}}{U^n S}\right)^{\mu} (N(d_3) - N(d_4))\right] - K e^{-rT} \sum_{n=-N}^{N} [\dots]_{\mu - 2,\; d_i - \sigma\sqrt T},\]

with \(\mu = 2b/\sigma^2 + 1\). The terms decay like \(e^{-2n^2 \ln^2(U/L)/\sigma^2 T}\), so a handful suffice. The knock-in is the vanilla option minus the knock-out.

financialkit.exotic_options.fixed_lookback_price(S, K, T, r, sigma, q=0.0, option_type='call', S_extreme=None)[source]#

Conze-Viswanathan (1991) fixed-strike lookback: \((\max S - K)^+\) (call) or \((K - \min S)^+\) (put).

For the call with \(K \ge S_{\max}\),

\[c = S e^{(b-r)T} N(d_1) - K e^{-rT} N(d_2) + S e^{-rT} \frac{\sigma^2}{2b}\left[-\left(\frac{S}{K}\right)^{-2b/\sigma^2} N\!\left(d_1 - \frac{2b\sqrt T}{\sigma}\right) + e^{bT} N(d_1)\right];\]

for \(K < S_{\max}\) the call locks in \(e^{-rT}(S_{\max} - K)\) and adds the same expression with \(S_{\max}\) in place of \(K\). The put mirrors it in the minimum.

financialkit.exotic_options.variance_swap_strike(strikes, call_prices, put_prices, S, T, r, q=0.0)[source]#

Fair variance strike from out-of-the-money option prices (Demeterfi, Derman, Kamal and Zou 1999).

With \(K_0\) the largest strike at or below the forward \(F = S e^{(r-q)T}\), puts are used below \(K_0\), calls above and their average at \(K_0\), and

\[K_{\mathrm{var}} = \frac{2 e^{rT}}{T} \sum_i \frac{\Delta K_i}{K_i^2} Q(K_i) - \frac{1}{T}\left(\frac{F}{K_0} - 1\right)^2,\]

with \(\Delta K_i\) half the distance between the neighbouring strikes. The last term is the second-order expansion of the forward adjustment above. The strike range truncates the integrals, so the strip should reach well into both wings.

Parameters:

  • strikes (ArrayLike) – Increasing strikes.

  • call_prices (ArrayLike) – Call and put prices at those strikes.

  • put_prices (ArrayLike) – Call and put prices at those strikes.

  • S (float) – Spot, maturity and rate.

  • T (float) – Spot, maturity and rate.

  • r (float) – Spot, maturity and rate.

  • q (float) – Continuous dividend yield.

Return type:

VarianceSwapResult

Returns:

VarianceSwapResult

Examples

Under Black-Scholes the fair variance is \(\sigma^2\):

>>> import numpy as np
>>> from financialkit.options import black_scholes_price
>>> K = np.arange(20.0, 400.0, 1.0)
>>> c, p = (black_scholes_price(100.0, K, 1.0, 0.05, 0.2, option_type=t) for t in ("call", "put"))
>>> round(variance_swap_strike(K, c, p, 100.0, 1.0, 0.05).vol_strike, 4)
0.2
