financialkit.monte_carlo#

financialkit.monte_carlo hub · History · Examples · API reference

Crude Monte Carlo with standard errors and confidence intervals, Box-Muller normal variates and option prices by simulation through financialkit.integrators; variance reduction by stratification (Neyman allocation), importance sampling, antithetic variates, Latin hypercube sampling, regression control variates and moment matching; quasi-Monte Carlo with van der Corput, Halton and Sobol points (scipy.stats.qmc), exact and \(L^2\) star discrepancies, the Koksma-Hlawka bound, Owen-scrambled randomized QMC and Brownian-bridge path construction; likelihood-ratio and pathwise Greeks; and Giles’s adaptive multilevel Monte Carlo.

financialkit.monte_carlo: Monte Carlo estimation, variance reduction, quasi-Monte Carlo and Greeks.

Crude Monte Carlo after Metropolis and Ulam, with standard errors and confidence intervals on every estimate; Box-Muller normal variates; and Boyle’s option prices under Black-Scholes, simulated through financialkit.integrators. Variance reduction: Neyman’s stratified sampling, importance sampling by a mean shift, antithetic variates, Latin hypercube sampling (scipy.stats.qmc), regression control variates and Barraquand’s moment matching. Quasi-Monte Carlo: van der Corput, Halton and Sobol sequences, exact and \(L^2\) star discrepancies with the Koksma-Hlawka bound, Owen-scrambled randomized QMC with error bars, and high-dimensional path integrals with and without Brownian-bridge ordering. Greeks by the likelihood-ratio and pathwise methods, and Giles’s adaptive multilevel Monte Carlo.

financialkit.monte_carlo.antithetic_estimate(f, n_pairs, dim=None, rng=None, confidence=0.95)[source]#

Antithetic-variates estimate of \(E[f(Z)]\), \(Z \sim N(0, I)\) (Hammersley-Morton 1956).

Each normal draw is paired with its mirror image and the estimator averages the pair means \(\tfrac12[f(Z) + f(-Z)]\), which are i.i.d. Per evaluation the variance falls by the factor \(1 + \rho\), with \(\rho = \operatorname{corr}(f(Z), f(-Z))\), so it helps exactly when \(f\) is monotone.

Parameters:

  • f (Callable[[ndarray], ArrayLike]) – Vectorised integrand of normals of shape (n,) (dim=None) or (n, dim).

  • n_pairs (int) – Number of antithetic pairs; the cost is 2 * n_pairs.

  • dim (int | None) – Dimension of \(Z\).

  • rng (Union[Generator, int, None]) – Generator or seed.

  • confidence (float) – Coverage of the confidence interval.

Return type:

MonteCarloResult

Returns:

MonteCarloResult

Examples

For a linear integrand the pairs cancel exactly:

>>> antithetic_estimate(lambda z: 3.0 + z, 1000, rng=0).std_error < 1e-12

financialkit.monte_carlo.control_variate_estimate(y, controls, control_means, confidence=0.95)[source]#

Control-variate estimate of \(E[Y]\) from controls with known means (Lavenberg-Welch 1981).

The coefficients \(\beta\) are the least-squares slopes of \(Y\) on the controls \(X\), and

\[\hat\mu = \bar Y - \beta^\top (\bar X - \mu_X), \qquad \operatorname{Var}\hat\mu \approx (1 - R^2) \frac{s_Y^2}{n}.\]

The standard error uses the regression residuals with n - k - 1 degrees of freedom. Discounted \(S_T\) (mean \(S e^{-qT}\)) is the classic control for an option payoff.

Parameters:

  • y (ArrayLike) – Samples of the target.

  • controls (ArrayLike) – Samples of the controls, drawn jointly with y.

  • control_means (ArrayLike) – Exact expectations of the controls.

  • confidence (float) – Coverage of the confidence interval.

Return type:

ControlVariateResult

Returns:

ControlVariateResult

Examples

>>> import numpy as np
>>> x = np.random.default_rng(0).standard_normal(10_000)
>>> res = control_variate_estimate(np.exp(x), x, 0.0)
>>> res.covers(np.exp(0.5)), res.std_error < res.crude_std_error
(True, True)

Importance-sampling estimate of \(E[f(Z)]\), \(Z \sim N(0, I)\), by a mean shift.

Sampling \(Z \sim N(\theta, I)\) instead and weighting by the likelihood ratio

financialkit.monte_carlo.multilevel_monte_carlo(S, K, T, r, sigma, q=0.0, option_type='call', eps=0.05, M=2, L_min=2, L_max=10, N0=2000, rng=None, confidence=0.95)[source]#

European option by Giles’s adaptive multilevel Monte Carlo, to root-mean-square error eps.

The algorithm of Giles (2008), Sec. 5: start with levels 0 .. L_min and N0 samples each; set the optimal sample sizes

\[N_l = \left\lceil 2\varepsilon^{-2} \sqrt{V_l / C_l} \sum_k \sqrt{V_k C_k} \right\rceil,\]

which split \(\varepsilon^2\) evenly between variance and squared bias; top up any level that is short; and once none is, add a level unless the bias estimate \(\max(|\hat Y_L|, |\hat Y_{L-1}| / M) / (M - 1)\) (weak order 1) is below \(\varepsilon / \sqrt 2\).

Neyman’s optimal allocation \(n_h \propto p_h \sigma_h\) of n samples to strata.

financialkit.monte_carlo.star_discrepancy(points)[source]#

Exact star discrepancy \(D_N^*\) of a point set in one or two dimensions.

\[D_N^* = \sup_{a \in [0,1]^d} \left| \frac{\#\{x_i \in [0, a)\}}{N} - \prod_j a_j \right|.\]

In one dimension \(D_N^* = \frac{1}{2N} + \max_i |x_{(i)} - \frac{2i - 1}{2N}|\) (Niederreiter 1992, Thm. 2.6). In two the supremum is attained on the grid of point coordinates (and 1), which is searched with closed and open boxes in \(O(N^2)\); coordinates are assumed distinct.

Parameters:

float

Returns:

float

>>> N = 10
>>> round(star_discrepancy((2 * np.arange(1, N + 1) - 1) / (2 * N)), 12)

financialkit.monte_carlo.stratified_estimate(f, n, n_strata=10, allocation='neyman', n_pilot=200, rng=None, confidence=0.95)[source]#

Stratified estimate of \(E[f(U)]\), \(U \sim U[0, 1)\), over equal-probability strata.

Stratum \(h\) is \([h/H, (h+1)/H)\). The estimator and its variance are

\[\hat\mu = \sum_h p_h \bar f_h, \qquad \operatorname{Var}\hat\mu = \sum_h p_h^2 \frac{s_h^2}{n_h},\]

which never exceeds the crude variance under proportional allocation and is smallest under Neyman’s allocation (Neyman 1934). A one-dimensional uniform drives any Black-Scholes payoff through norm.ppf, so this stratifies the terminal price.

Parameters:

  • f (Callable[[ndarray], ArrayLike]) – Vectorised integrand of uniforms of shape (n,).

  • n (int) – Samples after the pilot.

  • n_strata (int) – Number of strata \(H\).

  • allocation (str) – Neyman’s allocation uses the pilot’s within-stratum standard deviations; proportional gives \(n_h = n / H\).

  • n_pilot (int) – Pilot samples for Neyman’s allocation, spread evenly over the strata; their cost is included in n_samples.

  • rng (Union[Generator, int, None]) – Generator or seed.

  • confidence (float) – Coverage of the confidence interval.

Return type:

StratifiedResult
