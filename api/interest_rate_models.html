financialkit.interest_rate_models#

financialkit.interest_rate_models hub · History · Examples · API reference

Equilibrium short rates: Merton’s Gaussian rate, Vasicek and Cox-Ingersoll-Ross with Feller’s condition, and Duffie-Kan affine models priced through their Riccati ODEs; curve-fitted models: Ho-Lee, Hull-White, the two-factor G2++ and the Black-Derman-Toy tree, all taking a financialkit.fixed_income yield curve; Feynman-Kac bond prices by Monte Carlo through financialkit.integrators; Jamshidian’s bond options, coupon bond options and swaptions; caps, floors and swaptions by Black’s formula (financialkit.options.black76_price()); the futures convexity adjustment; and the Heath-Jarrow-Morton drift condition with forward-curve simulation.

financialkit.interest_rate_models: short-rate models, curve-fitted models, HJM and rate options.

Equilibrium short rates: Merton’s arithmetic Brownian rate, Vasicek and Cox-Ingersoll-Ross (with Feller’s condition and exact noncentral chi-square sampling), and Duffie-Kan affine models of any dimension priced through their Riccati ODEs (scipy.integrate.solve_ivp()). Curve-fitted models take an initial YieldCurve: Ho-Lee, Hull-White, the two-factor G2++ and the Black-Derman-Toy lognormal tree (calibrated by forward induction). Every closed-form bond price is checked against Feynman-Kac Monte Carlo through financialkit.integrators.

Options: zero-coupon bond options in any Gaussian model (Jamshidian 1989, via the financialkit.options.black76_price() implementation), coupon bond options and swaptions by Jamshidian’s decomposition, caps and floors by Black’s formula on forward rates (the Brace-Gatarek-Musiela market model) or as bond options, Black swaptions under the swap market model, and the futures convexity adjustment. The Heath-Jarrow-Morton framework: the no-arbitrage drift and one-factor forward-curve simulation, with and without it.

class financialkit.interest_rate_models.CIRModel(r0, kappa, theta, sigma)[source]#

Bases: ShortRateModel

Cox, Ingersoll and Ross’s (1985) square-root short rate.

\[dr = \kappa(\theta - r)\,dt + \sigma\sqrt{r}\,dW\]

Volatility falls with the rate, so (under the Feller condition) rates stay positive. Bond prices are exponential-affine,

\[B = \frac{2(e^{\gamma\tau} - 1)}{(\gamma + \kappa)(e^{\gamma\tau} - 1) + 2\gamma}, \quad A = \left[\frac{2\gamma e^{(\kappa + \gamma)\tau/2}}{(\gamma + \kappa)(e^{\gamma\tau} - 1) + 2\gamma}\right]^{2\kappa\theta/\sigma^2},\]

with \(\gamma = \sqrt{\kappa^2 + 2\sigma^2}\) and \(P = A e^{-Br}\). The transition is a scaled noncentral chi-square, and the stationary law a gamma distribution with shape \(2\kappa\theta/\sigma^2\) and scale \(\sigma^2/(2\kappa)\).

Parameters:

  • r0 (float) – Initial rate (non-negative), mean-reversion speed, long-run mean and volatility.

  • kappa (float) – Initial rate (non-negative), mean-reversion speed, long-run mean and volatility.

  • theta (float) – Initial rate (non-negative), mean-reversion speed, long-run mean and volatility.

  • sigma (float) – Initial rate (non-negative), mean-reversion speed, long-run mean and volatility.

Examples

>>> m = CIRModel(0.06, 0.1, 0.1, 0.11)
>>> round(float(m.zero_coupon_bond(10.0)), 4)
0.5039
>>> round(m.feller_ratio, 3)
1.653

class financialkit.interest_rate_models.G2PlusPlusModel(curve, a, sigma, b, eta, rho)[source]#

Bases: GaussianShortRateModel

The G2++ model, fitted to an initial curve.

Bond prices given the factors \((x, y)\) at time \(t\) are

\[P(t, T) = \frac{P(0, T)}{P(0, t)} \exp\left\{\tfrac12[V(t, T) - V(0, T) + V(0, t)] - B_a(\tau) x - B_b(\tau) y\right\},\]

with \(B_k(\tau) = (1 - e^{-k\tau})/k\) and \(V(t, T)\) the variance of \(\int_t^T (x + y)\,du\) (Brigo-Mercurio eq. 4.10).

Parameters:

  • curve (YieldCurve | float) – Initial term structure.

  • a (float) – Mean reversion and volatility of the first factor.

  • sigma (float) – Mean reversion and volatility of the first factor.

  • b (float) – Mean reversion and volatility of the second factor.

  • eta (float) – Mean reversion and volatility of the second factor.

  • rho (float) – Correlation of the two Brownian drivers, in (-1, 1).

Examples

>>> m = G2PlusPlusModel(0.03, 0.5, 0.01, 0.05, 0.008, -0.7)
>>> round(float(m.zero_coupon_bond(10.0)), 6) == round(float(np.exp(-0.3)), 6)
True

class financialkit.interest_rate_models.HoLeeModel(curve, sigma)[source]#

Bases: _CurveFittedGaussian

Ho and Lee’s (1986) arbitrage-free model, in its continuous-time limit.

\[dr = \theta(t)\,dt + \sigma\,dW, \qquad \theta(t) = f_T(0, t) + \sigma^2 t,\]

where \(f(0, t)\) is today’s instantaneous forward curve. The drift is whatever it takes to reprice every zero-coupon bond, so the model fits any initial curve exactly. Bonds at later dates are

\[P(t, T) = \frac{P(0, T)}{P(0, t)} \exp\left(\tau f(0, t) - \tfrac12\sigma^2 t \tau^2 - \tau r(t)\right).\]

Ho and Lee built it as a binomial tree of whole discount curves; it is Merton’s Gaussian rate with a curve-fitting drift.

Parameters:

  • curve (YieldCurve | float) – Initial term structure (a float is a flat rate).

  • sigma (float) – Absolute volatility of the short rate.

Examples

>>> from financialkit.fixed_income import NelsonSiegelCurve
>>> curve = NelsonSiegelCurve(0.05, -0.02, 0.01, 2.0)
>>> bool(np.isclose(HoLeeModel(curve, 0.01).zero_coupon_bond(7.0), curve.discount(7.0)))
True

class financialkit.interest_rate_models.HullWhiteModel(curve, a, sigma)[source]#

Bases: _CurveFittedGaussian

Hull and White’s (1990) extended Vasicek model.

\[dr = (\theta(t) - a r)\,dt + \sigma\,dW, \qquad \theta(t) = f_T(0, t) + a f(0, t) + \frac{\sigma^2}{2a}(1 - e^{-2at}).\]

Vasicek’s mean reversion with Ho-Lee’s curve-fitting drift: the initial curve is matched exactly and volatility of long rates decays like \(B(\tau)/\tau\). The short rate is \(r(t) = x(t) + \varphi(t)\) with \(x\) an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process started at 0 and \(\varphi(t) = f(0, t) + \tfrac{\sigma^2}{2a^2}(1 - e^{-at})^2\).

Parameters:

  • curve (YieldCurve | float) – Initial term structure (a float is a flat rate).

  • a (float) – Mean-reversion speed; a = 0 is Ho-Lee.

  • sigma (float) – Absolute volatility of the short rate.

Examples

>>> hw = HullWhiteModel(0.04, 0.1, 0.01)
>>> round(float(hw.zero_coupon_bond(5.0)), 6)
0.818731

class financialkit.interest_rate_models.MertonShortRateModel(r0, mu, sigma)[source]#

Bases: GaussianShortRateModel

Merton’s (1973) Gaussian short rate, \(dr = \mu\,dt + \sigma\,dW\).

The first stochastic interest rate in continuous time, from Section 6 of Merton’s “Theory of Rational Option Pricing”. Bond prices are

\[P(t, T) = \exp\left(-r\tau - \tfrac12 \mu\tau^2 + \tfrac16 \sigma^2\tau^3\right), \quad \tau = T - t,\]

so the cubic convexity term eventually makes long yields fall without bound: rates without mean reversion cannot carry a long curve.

Parameters:

  • r0 (float) – Initial short rate.

  • mu (float) – Constant drift of the short rate, per year.

  • sigma (float) – Absolute volatility of the short rate.

Examples

>>> m = MertonShortRateModel(0.05, 0.0, 0.0)
>>> round(float(m.zero_coupon_bond(2.0)), 6)
0.904837

class financialkit.interest_rate_models.VasicekModel(r0, kappa, theta, sigma)[source]#

financialkit.interest_rate_models.black_derman_toy_tree(curve, sigma, maturity, n_steps)[source]#

Calibrate a Black-Derman-Toy lognormal binomial short-rate tree to a zero curve.

At step \(i\) the rates are \(r_{i,j} = U_i e^{\sigma_i\sqrt{\Delta t}\,(2j - i)}\), \(j = 0, \dots, i\), with probability 1/2 on each branch, so the log short rate has volatility \(\sigma_i\) and rates stay positive. Each median level \(U_i\) is solved by scipy.optimize.brentq() so that the tree reprices the bond maturing at \(t_{i+1}\),

\[\sum_j Q_{i,j} e^{-r_{i,j}\Delta t} = P(0, t_{i+1}),\]

where the Arrow-Debreu prices \(Q\) are carried forward one level at a time (Jamshidian’s 1991 forward induction). This is the short-rate-volatility form of the model; Black, Derman and Toy’s original also matched a term structure of yield volatilities.

Parameters:

  • sigma (float or array_like, shape (n_steps,)) – Volatility of the log short rate at each step.

  • maturity (float) – Tree horizon in years.

  • n_steps (int) – Number of steps.
