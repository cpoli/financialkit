financialkit.stochastic_volatility#

financialkit.stochastic_volatility hub · History · Examples · API reference

Local volatility: Cox’s constant elasticity of variance with Schroder’s noncentral chi-square prices, Dupire’s local volatility from a price surface, and its simulation through financialkit.integrators.euler_maruyama(). Stochastic volatility: the Hull-White series and mixing Monte Carlo, and the Heston model with its two-probability formula. Jumps: Merton’s jump diffusion (series formula), variance gamma (Madan-Seneta, Madan-Carr-Chang), Kou’s double-exponential jumps and Bates. Fourier pricing for every characteristic-function model: Carr-Madan’s FFT, Lewis’s single integral and the COS method. Smiles: Hagan’s SABR formula and calibration, Gatheral’s SVI with Durrleman’s condition, and Lee’s moment formula. Simulation: full-truncation, partial-truncation and Higham-Mao Euler schemes and Andersen’s quadratic-exponential scheme for Heston.

financialkit.stochastic_volatility: beyond constant volatility.

Local volatility: Cox’s constant elasticity of variance with Schroder’s noncentral chi-square prices, and Dupire’s local volatility recovered from a price surface and simulated through financialkit.integrators.euler_maruyama(). Stochastic volatility: Hull and White’s mixing formula, and the Heston model with its two-probability formula. Jumps: Merton’s jump diffusion (series formula), the Madan-Seneta and Madan-Carr-Chang variance gamma process, Kou’s double-exponential jumps and Bates’s Heston-with-jumps. Fourier pricing for every characteristic-function model: Carr-Madan’s FFT, Lewis’s single integral and Fang-Oosterlee’s COS method. Smiles: Hagan’s SABR formula and calibration, Gatheral’s SVI with Durrleman’s arbitrage check, and Lee’s moment formula for the wings. Simulation: truncated Euler schemes (Lord-Koekkoek-van Dijk) and Andersen’s quadratic-exponential scheme for Heston.

class financialkit.stochastic_volatility.VarianceGammaModel(sigma, nu, theta=0.0)[source]#

Bases: CharacteristicFunctionModel

Variance gamma: Brownian motion with drift run on a gamma clock (Madan-Seneta 1990, Madan-Carr-Chang 1998).

\(X_T = \omega T + \theta G_T + \sigma W_{G_T}\), with \(G_T \sim \Gamma(T/\nu, \nu)\) and \(\omega = \ln(1 - \theta\nu - \tfrac12\sigma^2\nu)/\nu\) so that \(E[e^{X_T}] = 1\). theta = 0 is the symmetric Madan-Seneta process; theta < 0 skews it to the left.

Parameters:

  • sigma (float) – Volatility of the Brownian motion in business time.

  • nu (float) – Variance rate of the gamma clock (excess kurtosis).

  • theta (float) – Drift in business time (skewness).

Examples

>>> m = VarianceGammaModel(sigma=0.12, nu=0.2, theta=-0.14)
>>> round(m.price(100.0, 90.0, 1.0, 0.1), 6)  # Fang-Oosterlee (2008), Table 7
19.099355

financialkit.stochastic_volatility.carr_madan_fft(model, S, T, r, q=0.0, alpha=1.5, N=4096, eta=0.25)[source]#

Call prices on a grid of log strikes by the Carr-Madan (1999) FFT.

The damped call \(c(k) = e^{\alpha k} C(k)\) has Fourier transform

\[\psi(v) = \frac{e^{-rT} \phi_{\ln S_T}(v - (\alpha + 1) i)}{\alpha^2 + \alpha - v^2 + i (2\alpha + 1) v},\]

and the inverse transform at \(N\) log strikes spaced \(\lambda = 2\pi/(N\eta)\) around \(\ln F\) is one FFT of \(\psi\) sampled at \(v_j = j\eta\) with Simpson weights.

Parameters:

  • model (CharacteristicFunctionModel)

  • S (float)

  • T (float)

  • r (float)

  • q (float)

  • alpha (float) – Damping exponent; \(E[S_T^{\alpha + 1}]\) must be finite.

  • N (int) – Number of grid points (a power of two is fastest).

  • eta (float) – Spacing of the integration grid in \(v\).

Return type:

FFTResult

Returns:

FFTResult

Examples

>>> from financialkit.options import black_scholes_price
>>> res = carr_madan_fft(BlackScholesModel(0.2), 100.0, 1.0, 0.05)
>>> i = abs(res.strikes - 100.0).argmin()

financialkit.stochastic_volatility.cev_price(S, K, T, r, sigma, beta, q=0.0, option_type='call')[source]#

European option prices under Cox’s CEV model (Schroder 1989).

For \(dS = (r - q) S\,dt + \sigma S^{\beta}\,dW\) with \(0 < \beta < 1\), writing \(Q(z; k, \lambda)\) for the noncentral chi-square CDF,

\[C = S e^{-qT}\left[1 - Q(a; b + 2, c)\right] - K e^{-rT} Q(c; b, a),\]

with \(a = (K e^{-(r-q)T})^{2(1-\beta)} / ((1-\beta)^2 v)\), \(b = 1/(1 - \beta)\), \(c = S^{2(1-\beta)} / ((1-\beta)^2 v)\) and \(v = \sigma^2 [e^{2(r-q)(\beta-1)T} - 1] / (2(r-q)(\beta-1))\) (\(\sigma^2 T\) when \(r = q\)). For \(\beta > 1\) the roles swap: \(C = S e^{-qT}[1 - Q(c; -b, a)] - K e^{-rT} Q(a; 2 - b, c)\). Puts follow by parity.

Parameters:

  • S (ArrayLike) – Spot, strike, maturity and risk-free rate.

  • K (ArrayLike) – Spot, strike, maturity and risk-free rate.

  • T (ArrayLike) – Spot, strike, maturity and risk-free rate.

  • r (ArrayLike) – Spot, strike, maturity and risk-free rate.

  • sigma (float) – Volatility scale; the local volatility is \(\sigma S^{\beta - 1}\).

  • beta (float) – Elasticity exponent, positive and not 1 (that is Black-Scholes).

  • q (ArrayLike) – Dividend yield.

  • option_type (str)

Return type:

ndarray | float

Returns:

float or ndarray

Examples

Hull, 10th ed., Section 27.1, uses the same formula; with \(\beta = 0.5\) and \(\sigma = 2\) the at-the-money local volatility is 20%, and the price is close to Black-Scholes at 20%:

>>> round(cev_price(100.0, 100.0, 1.0, 0.05, 2.0, 0.5), 4)
10.4539

European option prices by the Fang-Oosterlee (2008) COS method.

With \(y = \ln(S_T/K)\) truncated to \([a_y, b_y]\) and \(u_k = k\pi/(b_y - a_y)\),

\[V = e^{-rT} \sum_{k=0}^{N-1}{}' \operatorname{Re}\!\left[\phi(u_k) e^{-i u_k a}\right] V_k,\]

where \(a = a_y - \ln(F/K)\) is the interval’s left end for \(X_T\), the prime halves the first term, and \(V_k\) are the cosine coefficients of the payoff, in closed form. Puts are priced by the series (their payoff is bounded) and calls by parity, as Fang and Oosterlee recommend.

  • S (float) – Spot.

  • T (float) – Maturity, risk-free rate and dividend yield.

  • r (float) – Maturity, risk-free rate and dividend yield.

  • q (float) – Maturity, risk-free rate and dividend yield.

  • option_type (str)

  • N (int | None) – Number of cosine terms; by default cos_terms() of the interval.

  • L (float) – Truncation width, see truncation_range().

Return type:

ndarray | float

Returns:

float or ndarray

Examples

financialkit.stochastic_volatility.hull_white_price(S, K, T, r, sigma0, xi, q=0.0, option_type='call')[source]#

Hull-White (1987) series for an option under uncorrelated lognormal variance.

With \(dV = \xi V\,dW\) independent of the stock and \(\bar V = \frac1T\int_0^T V_t\,dt\), the price is \(E[C_{BS}(\bar V)]\); expanding about \(V_0 = \sigma_0^2\),
