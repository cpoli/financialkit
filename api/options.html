financialkit.options#

financialkit.options hub · History · Examples · API reference

Closed-form European prices: Bachelier’s normal model, Black-Scholes-Merton with a dividend yield, Black-76 on forwards and futures, and Garman-Kohlhagen for currencies, all broadcasting over array inputs; put-call parity and Merton’s no-arbitrage bounds; analytic Greeks checked against finite differences, and a discretely rebalanced delta hedge on paths from financialkit.integrators.gbm_paths(); implied volatility (scipy.optimize.brentq after a bounds check), the Brenner-Subrahmanyam approximation, the smile and the Breeden-Litzenberger density; Cox-Ross-Rubinstein, Rendleman-Bartter, Leisen-Reimer and Boyle trinomial trees with American early exercise, and the Barone-Adesi-Whaley approximation.

financialkit.options: vanilla option pricing, Greeks, implied volatility and lattices.

Closed forms: Bachelier’s normal model, Black-Scholes-Merton with a dividend yield, Black-76 on forwards (the one implementation the rest of financialkit calls) and Garman-Kohlhagen for currencies, all broadcasting over array inputs; put-call parity and Merton’s no-arbitrage bounds; analytic Greeks, checked against finite differences, and a simulated discrete delta hedge built on financialkit.integrators.gbm_paths(). Implied volatility by scipy.optimize.brentq() after a bounds check, the Brenner-Subrahmanyam at-the-money shortcut, the smile, and the Breeden-Litzenberger risk-neutral density. Lattices: Cox-Ross-Rubinstein, Rendleman-Bartter and Leisen-Reimer binomial trees and Boyle’s trinomial tree (hand-written backward induction, with American early exercise and the exercise boundary), plus the Barone-Adesi-Whaley approximation.

class financialkit.options.TrinomialTree(n_steps=200, stretch=1.224744871391589)[source]#

Bases: LatticePricer

Boyle’s (1986) trinomial tree, \(u = e^{\lambda\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}}\), middle branch flat.

With \(M = e^{(r-q)\Delta t}\) and \(V = M^2(e^{\sigma^2\Delta t} - 1)\),

\[p_u = \frac{(V + M^2 - M)u - (M - 1)}{(u - 1)(u^2 - 1)}, \quad p_d = \frac{(V + M^2 - M)u^2 - (M - 1)u^3}{(u - 1)(u^2 - 1)}, \quad p_m = 1 - p_u - p_d,\]

which match the mean and variance of the next price exactly.

Parameters:

  • n_steps (int)

  • stretch (float) – Boyle’s \(\lambda > 1\); the default \(\sqrt{3/2}\) puts about a third of the probability on the middle branch.

Examples

>>> from financialkit.options import black_scholes_price
>>> tri = TrinomialTree(200).price(100.0, 100.0, 1.0, 0.05, 0.2).price
>>> bool(abs(tri - black_scholes_price(100.0, 100.0, 1.0, 0.05, 0.2)) < 0.01)
True

financialkit.options.bachelier_price(S, K, T, r, sigma_n, q=0.0, option_type='call')[source]#

Bachelier’s (1900) normal-model price of a European option.

The forward follows an arithmetic Brownian motion with absolute volatility \(\sigma_N\) (price units per square-root year), so

\[c = e^{-rT}\left[(F - K) N(d) + \sigma_N\sqrt{T}\, n(d)\right], \quad d = \frac{F - K}{\sigma_N \sqrt{T}},\]

and the put follows from parity. At the money, \(c = e^{-rT}\sigma_N\sqrt{T/(2\pi)}\). Bachelier’s own setting is r = q = 0.

Parameters:

  • S (ArrayLike) – Spot, strike, maturity (years) and risk-free rate.

  • K (ArrayLike) – Spot, strike, maturity (years) and risk-free rate.

  • T (ArrayLike) – Spot, strike, maturity (years) and risk-free rate.

  • r (ArrayLike) – Spot, strike, maturity (years) and risk-free rate.

  • sigma_n (ArrayLike) – Normal (absolute) volatility.

  • q (ArrayLike) – Continuous dividend yield.

  • option_type (str)

Return type:

ndarray | float

Returns:

float or ndarray

Examples

>>> import numpy as np
>>> price = bachelier_price(100.0, 100.0, 1.0, 0.0, 20.0)
>>> bool(np.isclose(price, 20.0 / np.sqrt(2 * np.pi)))
True

With \(\phi = \pm 1\) for a call/put:

\[\begin{split}\Delta = \phi e^{-qT} N(\phi d_1), \quad \Gamma = \frac{e^{-qT} n(d_1)}{S \sigma \sqrt{T}}, \quad \mathcal{V} = S e^{-qT} n(d_1) \sqrt{T}, \\ \Theta = -\frac{S e^{-qT} n(d_1) \sigma}{2\sqrt{T}} - \phi r K e^{-rT} N(\phi d_2) + \phi q S e^{-qT} N(\phi d_1), \\ \rho = \phi K T e^{-rT} N(\phi d_2), \quad \epsilon = -\phi S T e^{-qT} N(\phi d_1), \quad \text{vanna} = -e^{-qT} n(d_1) \frac{d_2}{\sigma}, \quad \text{volga} = \mathcal{V} \frac{d_1 d_2}{\sigma}.\end{split}\]

Black-Scholes-Merton price of a European option on a dividend-paying stock.

\[c = S e^{-qT} N(d_1) - K e^{-rT} N(d_2), \quad p = K e^{-rT} N(-d_2) - S e^{-qT} N(-d_1),\]

financialkit.options.brenner_subrahmanyam_vol(price, S, T, r=0.0, q=0.0)[source]#

Brenner-Subrahmanyam (1988) approximation for at-the-money-forward options.

For \(K = F\), \(c = S e^{-qT}[2N(\sigma\sqrt{T}/2) - 1] \approx S e^{-qT} \sigma \sqrt{T / (2\pi)}\), so

\[\sigma \approx \sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{T}} \frac{c}{S e^{-qT}},\]

with a relative error of order \(\sigma^2 T / 24\). At the forward, calls and puts have the same price, so either can be passed.

  • S (ArrayLike) – Spot, maturity, risk-free rate and dividend yield (r is not used by the formula and is accepted for a uniform signature).

  • r (ArrayLike) – Spot, maturity, risk-free rate and dividend yield (r is not used by the formula and is accepted for a uniform signature).

  • q (ArrayLike) – Spot, maturity, risk-free rate and dividend yield (r is not used by the formula and is accepted for a uniform signature).
