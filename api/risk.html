financialkit.risk: value at risk, expected shortfall, extreme values, backtests, copulas and drawdowns.

Value at risk by historical simulation, normal and Student-t closed forms, RiskMetrics delta-normal, the Cornish-Fisher expansion, full revaluation Monte Carlo (through financialkit.integrators), Boudoukh-Richardson-Whitelaw age weighting and filtered historical simulation; Roy’s safety-first criterion; Acerbi-Tasche expected shortfall, the coherence axioms and Euler risk allocation; block maxima and peaks over threshold (scipy.stats.genextreme, scipy.stats.genpareto); Kupiec and Christoffersen backtests and the Basel traffic light; Gaussian and t copulas with tail dependence; drawdowns and the Calmar ratio. Profits and losses go in with losses negative, and every risk figure comes out as a positive loss.

financialkit.risk.cornish_fisher_var(mu, sigma, skew, excess_kurtosis, alpha=0.99)[source]#

VaR from the Cornish-Fisher expansion of the quantile in skewness and kurtosis.

With \(z = \Phi^{-1}(1 - \alpha)\), skewness \(S\) and excess kurtosis \(K\), the adjusted quantile is

\[z_{cf} = z + \frac{(z^2 - 1) S}{6} + \frac{(z^3 - 3z) K}{24} - \frac{(2z^3 - 5z) S^2}{36},\]

and \(\mathrm{VaR} = -(\mu + \sigma z_{cf})\).

Parameters:

  • mu (ArrayLike) – First four moments of the profit and loss.

  • sigma (ArrayLike) – First four moments of the profit and loss.

  • skew (ArrayLike) – First four moments of the profit and loss.

  • excess_kurtosis (ArrayLike) – First four moments of the profit and loss.

  • alpha (ArrayLike) – Confidence level.

Return type:

ndarray | float

Returns:

float or ndarray

Examples

With no skew and no excess kurtosis it is the normal VaR.

>>> round(cornish_fisher_var(0.0, 1.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.99), 4)
2.3263
>>> round(cornish_fisher_var(0.0, 1.0, -0.5, 3.0, 0.99), 4)
3.3013

financialkit.risk.filtered_historical_var(pnl, sigma, sigma_forecast, alpha=0.99)[source]#

Filtered historical simulation (Barone-Adesi, Giannopoulos and Vosper 1999).

Each past outcome is divided by the volatility of its day, giving roughly i.i.d. standardized residuals \(z_t = x_t / \sigma_t\), and rescaled by tomorrow’s forecast: the scenarios are \(\hat\sigma_{n+1} z_t\). The empirical shape of the tail is kept while its scale follows current volatility.

Parameters:

  • pnl (ArrayLike) – Historical profits and losses.

  • sigma (ArrayLike) – Conditional volatility of each day, from any filter (EWMA, GARCH).

  • sigma_forecast (float) – Volatility forecast for the day the VaR is for.

  • alpha (float) – Confidence level.

Return type:

VaRResult

Returns:
